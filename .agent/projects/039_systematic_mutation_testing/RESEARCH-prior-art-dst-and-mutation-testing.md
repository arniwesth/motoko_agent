_Prior art shrinks the thesis that simulation and mutation complete each other._

The thesis is not new in its core: every element of it has a dated precedent, and the combination itself was published eleven days before this review. The four-way sort of half (b) has been a commercial hardware-verification product since **2007** (Certess Certitude, patent priority 2004) under the names Non-Activated / Non-Propagated / Non-Detected / Detected, with the same three measurements and the same routing of each class to stimulus, observability or checkers; Java unit-test research rebuilt it by diffing original and mutant runs in **2019** and **2024**. Half (a) is old in each of its three parts: mutants have graded generators since at least 2013 (a platform since 2023), property sets since 2014–2016, and corpus members since 2023. The pairing with DST by name is Antithesis's post of **2026-09-25** and its open skill, which already sorts each mutant into caught / never ran / ran-and-nothing-noticed and routes survivors to workload, oracle, mutant or property; two hobby Raft repositories (July and September 2026) grade DST invariants and search strategies against planted bugs. What the searches did not find is narrower: a same-seed, whole-trace comparison of each mutant against the unmutated run on a DST harness for software (a first-hand check found the word "seed" nowhere in the Antithesis skill); an operator-generated mutant population run against such a harness with a distribution of verdicts; kill sets per member of a fixed seed corpus; any measurement of how well "no difference on this corpus" predicts equivalence there; and any of it on a seeded simulation of an LLM agent loop. The evidence base is about 160 Exa searches by six researchers, no citation index, and several machine summaries that proved wrong on checking, so every "not found" below is weak evidence and every source read only as an abstract or summary is labelled as a lead.

## Mutants have graded properties, generators and corpora for a decade

Half (a) says mutants supply the ground truth a harness lacks for three things. Stated generally, each is established, and the literature that validates other oracle metrics treats mutation score as the reference they are validated against: checked coverage (2011), state field coverage (2025, Pearson 0.54 against mutation score on 273 representation invariants) and the coverage-minus-mutation-score scalar that Jain et al. named the "oracle gap" in 2023 are all justified by correlation with it ([Molina et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03071); [Jain et al. 2023, Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/gft8bs5ggy2)). The thesis's own term "oracle gap" therefore collides with a published definition.

**Invariant strength.** For QuickCheck-style properties this is a tool: MuCheck (2014) "can be used to evaluate the efficacy of QuickCheck property definitions", and FitSpec (2016) builds a kill matrix of properties against mutants, reads a survivor as "incompleteness of the property set", and derives minimal subsets and implications between properties from the same matrix ([MuCheck](https://www.uh.edu/serg/publications/issta14tool.pdf); [FitSpec](https://matela.com.br/fitspec.pdf)). Hughes did it by hand in 2019 with eight buggy search trees and a property-by-bug table ([Hughes 2019](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/517894/file/517894_Fulltext.pdf)). Model checking defined coverage this way in **1999**: flip a signal in a state, and if the property still holds that state is uncovered ([Hoskote et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1145/309847.309936)). Groce et al. carried that idea to real C verification harnesses (Mozilla's JavaScript engine, Linux RCU) and generate tests that "fail to kill a mutant but cover the mutated code" to show the limits of a harness ([Groce et al. 2018](https://agroce.github.io/asej18.pdf)). The strict kill the thesis's system uses, where a kill counts only when the check written for the broken rule fails, is also published: property-based mutation testing defines a mutant as "meaningfully-killed with respect to a property only if it causes the violation of that property" ([Bartocci et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13615)), BERTiMuS states that this notion "is stronger than the classical one" ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07553)), and Du, Palepu and Jones measured why it matters: crashes account for **up to 43.8% of killed mutants** in ten Java projects, and test oracles for as little as 11.8% of test failures in one ([Du et al., ISSTA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597926.3598090)). What the notes did not find is a kill matrix over invariants judged on the execution trace of a simulated system in reviewed literature; one researcher also found no checker paper in the linearizability or isolation literature that validates itself by mutation (Elle's full text was keyword-searched for it and has none) ([Kingsbury and Alvaro 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)).

**Generator quality.** Hriţcu et al. (2013) inject bugs one at a time into an abstract machine and report mean time to failure per generation strategy, then reuse the same bug set to compare formulations of the property, which is both axes of half (a) on one defect set ([Hriţcu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1409.0393)). Etna (2023) turned this into a platform whose stated principle is "Evaluate for the ground truth, not for proxy metrics"; its mutants are hand-written, its unit is a mutant-property pair known to fail, and it records no coverage, so it cannot separate "never reached" from "reached and missed" ([Keles et al., Etna journal version](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27002v3)). Fuzzing followed: Görz et al. (2023) call mutation analysis "the gold standard" and found the best fuzzer covers **30.9%** of mutants and detects 28.3% of those ([Görz et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.03075v3)). In simulation-based testing the same practice grades scenario samplers and scenario generators (muPRL 2024, read in part through Exa's copy, "is effective at discriminating strong from weak test generators") ([Thomas et al., Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/nw27rwph2ph)). For distributed systems the oldest match is Rutherford, Carzaniga and Wolf (2006/2008), who applied MuJava operators to a discrete-event simulation of a distributed system and ranked test suites by mutant score; they mutated the simulation acting as specification, not production code, and they judged kills by assertions and sanity checks instead of output comparison because "simulations of distributed systems are naturally nondeterministic" ([Rutherford et al. 2008](https://www.inf.usi.ch/carzaniga/papers/rcw_tse08.pdf)). Systems-venue tool papers do something weaker: one to three hand-seeded or flag-re-introduced bugs as a fixed target for comparing schedulers, as in P# at FAST 2016 ([Deligiannis et al. 2016](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/fast16/fast16-papers-deligiannis.pdf)). Gligoric et al. named that gap in 2013: concurrency testing techniques "have been mostly evaluated using programs with a small number of real or manually seeded concurrency bugs" ([Gligoric et al. 2013](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~gligoric/papers/GligoricETAL13Comutation.pdf)).

**Corpus value.** Mu2 (ISSTA 2023) is the direct precedent: "Inputs are saved to the corpus if they improve mutation score", judged by a differential oracle between mutant and original ([Vikram et al. 2023](https://rohan.padhye.org/files/mu2-issta23.pdf)). Görz et al. reuse a fuzzing corpus as a static suite that kills the trivial mutants and leaves the stubborn ones as targets. Kill-preserving test-suite reduction and Laurent et al.'s per-scenario mutant kills on a deterministic driving simulator are leads only (summaries and one abstract). No source in the notes uses mutant kill sets to score or minimise the members of a seed corpus for a deterministic simulator.

## Hardware has shipped the four-way sort since 2007

Half (b) claims that determinism gives mutation three measurements per mutant and that together they sort survivors four ways, each verdict feeding a different part of the harness. Hardware verification did exactly this first and said why in nearly the same words. Mark Hampton of Certess wrote in 2009: "To find a design bug, three things must occur ... 1. The bug must be activated ... 2. The bug must be propagated to an observable point ... 3. The bug must be detected; i.e. behavior is checked and a failure indicated", and noted that coverage addresses only the first ([Hampton 2009](https://www.eetimes.com/functional-qualification-a-technical-brief/)). Certitude launched on **7 May 2007** as "the first commercial functional" qualification product ([Certess press release](https://www.design-reuse.com/news/15804/certess-certitude-functional-qualification-electronic-designs.html)); the underlying patent has a priority date of 2004-07-30, though only its front matter was read ([US8311793B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8311793B2/en)). Brownell's DVCon 2013 paper defines the classes: a fault is Non-Activated if no test executes its line; **Non-Propagated** if "there was no difference observed at the 'output of the design' when compared to the same test with no fault inserted"; **Non-Detected** if there was a difference "but the test checkers did not report a failure" ([Brownell 2013](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/whos-watching-the-watchmen-the-time-has-come-to-objectively-measure-the-quality-of-your-verification-environment.pdf)). The same paper pins seeds for constrained-random tests, measures activation in one pass over the unmutated design, and routes the classes as the thesis does: Non-Activated means missing or over-constrained tests or dead code, Non-Propagated is fixed by "adding more seeds" or more observation points, Non-Detected indicates "weaknesses within current checkers". By 2012 the method ran on embedded C: on 14,000 lines of firmware and 6,781 faults STMicroelectronics reported **0.9% non-activated, 1.7% non-propagated, 10.2% non-detected, 78.5% detected** ([Bouvier et al. 2012](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/a-practical-approach-to-measuring-and-improving-the-functional-verification-of-embedded-software.pdf)). Große et al. (2016, read in part) applied the activate / propagate / detect sort to software tests on virtual prototypes by comparing an unmutated and a mutated run ([Große et al. 2016](https://agra.informatik.uni-bremen.de/doc/konf/2016ICCD_SWTestQualificationIPVP.pdf)).

Two details of the hardware record cut against the thesis's labels. Brownell says the boundary between Non-Propagated and Non-Detected is nothing but the declaration of where "output of the design" lies, and that practitioners read Non-Propagated first as "missing tests", with up to 30% of faults in that class in the worst cases; a vendor case study, read in part, attributes its residual cases to "redundant code" ([SpringSoft slides](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/38439320/certitudetm-functional-qualification-with-formal-verification)). The sources give no ratio between the two readings. Calling this class "equivalent on this corpus" names the benign reading; the hardware community's default is the other one.

The older hardware lines each hold a subset, which shows where the three-way split comes from. Fault simulation runs the same vectors on the good machine and each faulty machine, and "if the output patterns of one bad machine and the good machine are different, the fault is said to have been detected": the difference is the verdict, because the oracle is the good machine's response ([Cheng and Yu 1989](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00134011), partial read). Observability-based coverage (1996, abstract only) added an analytic estimate of propagation to statement coverage without injecting real mutants ([Devadas et al. 1996](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/569832)). The split into "differs" and "detected" appears only when the judge is weaker than a full output comparison: a testbench checker in Certitude, a safety mechanism in ISO 26262 fault campaigns, where a fault observed at an observation point but not caught is a "dangerous undetected" fault ([Ahuja et al.](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/formal-assisted-fault-campaign-for-iso26262-certification.pdf), partial read, year not verified). Model-checking coverage has one verdict per mutant and so merges "no difference" with "difference the specification does not see"; Chockler et al. leave the user to tell an incomplete specification from a redundant system ([Chockler et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44585-4_7)).

Software testing reached the same sort by its own route. The theory is Voas's PIE (1992; abstract only, and Exa's summary of it invented content) and the reach / infect / propagate / reveal model. The tools are Reneri (2019), which diffs recorded state of original and transformed runs and classifies undetected extreme mutants as no-infection, no-propagation or weak-oracle (**63% of 312** infect the local state; 42% of those propagate to an observable point) ([Vera-Pérez et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04770)), and Du, Palepu and Jones's "Ripples of a Mutation" (ICSE 2024), which instruments infection, propagation and test failure across **1.1 million mutated test runs** and finds that 18.5%–89.4% of surviving mutants "could potentially be killed based on infection or propagation information" ([Du et al., ICSE 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597503.3639179)). Both pay for nondeterminism: Reneri runs each side ten times and keeps only state that stayed unchanged; Ripples reruns the original ten times, excludes flaky tests and maintains an exclusion list of nondeterministic fields. Just et al. (2014) evaluate infection conditions on the unmutated run alone and find 3–42% of covered mutants "test-equivalent" ([Just et al. 2014](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/state-infection-issta2014.pdf)). Schuler and Zeller give the nearest published number for the thesis's "run differed" signal as an equivalence detector: on 140 hand-classified mutants, about 45% of undetected ones were equivalent, and coverage change used as the equivalence detector reached **75% precision and 56% recall** ([Schuler and Zeller 2013](https://www.st.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/files/schuler-stvrbis-2013.pdf)).

Other fields hold subsets. Fuzzing benchmarks instrument each injected bug to report reached, triggered and detected (Magma 2020; FixReverter 2022, where 24% of 7,910 injected bugs were reached and 8% triggered), and Görz et al. list comparing the mutant's behaviour with the original's as future work ([Hazimeh et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01120); [Zhang et al. 2022](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-zhang-zenong.pdf)). In cyber-physical simulation, DaMAT (TSE 2023) separates message type never exercised, mutation not applicable, and applied-but-live, and reads the last as "poor oracle quality" after confirming the outputs differ ([Viganò et al. 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TSE.2022.3213041)); Shin's 2026 vision paper proposes weakly killed (control trace diverges) versus strongly killed (safety constraint violated) and approximates never-weakly-killed as equivalent, then concedes that simulator nondeterminism "can cause natural state divergences, making it difficult to determine whether a mutant is killed" ([Shin 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)). That field measures the cost: hard-flaky rates of 1% to 74% across five driving-simulation setups, answered with repeated runs and a statistical kill rule ([Amini et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18768)).

On determinism as the enabler the notes disagree in emphasis, and both readings are correct for their literatures. For concurrent programs the point is old and explicit: Carver (1993; abstract only) names nondeterminism as the obstacle and defines deterministic execution mutation testing ([Carver 1993, Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/8dyvsfm9v9d)), and MuTMuT (2010) runs mutants under a scheduler-controlling model checker, reusing the set of mutants reached during the unmutated exploration ([Gligoric et al. 2010](https://mir.cs.illinois.edu/marinov/publications/GligoricETAL10MuTMuT.pdf)). The flaky-test and trace-diff literatures do not make it: Shi, Bell and Marinov measure mutation scores moving four percentage points between runs with 9% of mutant-test pairs unknown, and prescribe reruns, not deterministic execution ([Shi et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3293882.3330568)); the Papadakis survey's 294,000 characters contain no treatment of deterministic execution as a remedy ([Papadakis et al. 2019](https://mutationtesting.uni.lu/survey.pdf)). The same literature carries the strongest objection to a same-seed differential. Gligoric et al. (2013) argue that running the same schedules on mutant and original "cannot be done" in general, because "the changes in the mutant may enable or disable some schedules". A seeded simulator always yields a legal run of the mutant, but once the mutant changes which events are enabled or how many random draws are consumed, the same seed no longer denotes the same world. No source measures how often that happens; this is one researcher's inference, not a sourced fact. A second caution is sourced, at abstract level: mutations themselves induce flakiness, in 54% of 28 Python projects ([Test Flimsiness, ICSE 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744916.3773125)).

## Antithesis and two Raft repositories reached DST first

Before September 2026 the documented DST practice was reachability instrumentation, ad hoc bug seeding and post-mortems of escaped bugs. FoundationDB's `TEST` macros count how many simulation runs reached a declared condition ([Zhou et al. 2021](https://www.foundationdb.org/files/fdb-paper.pdf)); Antithesis's documentation recommends "brown M&Ms", known bugs deliberately left in a build ([Antithesis docs](https://antithesis.com/docs/best_practices/is_antithesis_working/)); and TigerBeetle's 2025 analysis of a bug Jepsen found states the world-gap / oracle-gap split outright: either "the fuzzer workloads never generate an input which triggers the bug" or they do "but the verification is incorrect (or incomplete)" ([TigerBeetle 2025](https://tigerbeetle.com/blog/2025-06-06-fuzzer-blind-spots-meet-jepsen/)). The FoundationDB, ShardStore and Dropbox texts were keyword-searched and contain no mutation or bug seeding; negatives for Resonate, RisingWave, MadSim, Turmoil, Shuttle, Polar Signals, WarpStream, sled and S2 rest on machine summaries only and are leads.

Antithesis changed that on **2026-09-25**. Rohan Padhye's post describes "a new agent skill to perform mutation testing, injecting artificial bugs to validate or refine Antithesis tests until properties are falsifiable", reports each attempt as "Artificial bug was caught", "Buggy code never ran", "Bug ran, nothing noticed" or "No usable result", and gives a campaign on rqlite: **19 mutant injections across 46 runs** of 15–60 minutes, 11 of 13 safety properties falsified, with survivors answered by changes such as "Added node stops to the fault mix" and "Added a check inside rqlite" ([Padhye 2026](https://antithesis.com/blog/2026/mutation-testing/)). The skill (version string 2026-09-23) states the purpose as "Validate the *oracle*, not the system under test", requires a green baseline, puts a marker in each mutant patch "proving the buggy code executed", and has a verdict ladder that separates bad workload (nothing reaches the mutated site), rarity (site reached, divergence condition never occurred), bad mutant ("divergence masked or converged before observation"), bad oracle ("the predicate tolerates it") and bad property ([SKILL.md](https://github.com/antithesishq/antithesis-skills/blob/main/antithesis-mutation-testing/SKILL.md); [sweep-and-verdicts.md](https://github.com/antithesishq/antithesis-skills/blob/main/antithesis-mutation-testing/references/sweep-and-verdicts.md)). It also has anti-gaming rules, a "collateral damage" class for properties other than the target, and an explicit rejection of operator mutants for this setting: "simple syntactic changes are not good mutations because they mostly lead to run-time panics and short-circuit the validation of distributed safety properties". Half (a) for invariant strength, the reach measurement, the split of "ran, nothing noticed" into masked divergence and tolerant predicate, and the routing of verdicts to harness parts are all there.

Because two researchers left open whether the skill compares mutant and original on one seed, the coordinator checked all eight markdown files of the skill at commit `1fd8470d` on 2026-10-06 by keyword search. **The word "seed" occurs in none of them.** The baseline is a gate and a control ("A run of the unpatched SUT built from the same source as every mutant"), runs are samples ("No reach in one 15-minute randomized run is a sample, not proof"), and searches for trace or run diffing find only source diffs. So the skill takes reach and divergence from an instrumented marker and from the agent's analysis of a run, not from a paired comparison.

A second pass the same day read all eight files cover to cover, and it changes what can be claimed as different. The finding above stands, and the design explains it: a run is a search over many histories ("the mutant ran in forty histories where nothing failed, and the property failed in a forty-first where the mutant's branch was never taken"), divergence is a marker the mutant's author places "where the mutation actually diverges", and propagation and detection are traced by reading code before the run ("A trace is a prediction, not a result"). Three things the thesis's source system also does are in the skill and are therefore not distinct. It records a predicted verdict per mutant before the run and whether the actual verdict matched. Its kill is strict: the targeted property must fail "in a history carrying the mutant's marker, for a reason the log ties to the divergence", other properties firing are "collateral damage" credited only under a stated test, and a startup crash is "too blatant". And it names the circular kill: "Re-keying an assertion to the mutant that will then validate it proves nothing". Three statements mark where it stops. It designs "One mutant per property", and says of a catalog rebuilt from existing assertions that a sweep "cannot answer *is anything important unasserted?*, because there is nothing to mutate for a property nobody wrote". It treats a mutant that removes its own evidence as a design error ("it hides its own effect and survives for the wrong reason"). And its cost per mutant is a search, budgeted at about 5N runs for N properties at "roughly 45 minutes for a 15-minute run" plus an image build each. Still unexamined: the shell scripts under `assets/mutation-testing/`, and whether the hosted platform offers seed-pinned replay that the skill does not mention.

Two unreviewed GitHub repositories, each with zero stars and one author, do the hand-planted DST version of half (a). `asp53826/dst-harness` (created 2026-07-31) plants twelve Raft defects one at a time in a seed-deterministic simulator and tabulates mutant, first failing seed (1 to 485) and "caught by"; two defects "are caught only by the linearizability checker, and never by any Raft invariant", a crash is reported as `crash` "rather than folded into the invariant column", and a survivor (`single_slot`, 400 seeds) exposed a fault schedule that "essentially never" hit a 4 ms fsync window ([dst-harness](https://github.com/asp53826/dst-harness)). `Aditya2k5here/counterexample` (created 2026-09-05) compares three search strategies on eight seeded Raft defects and reports a negative result: an apparent advantage at two seeds "was noise" at five ([counterexample](https://github.com/Aditya2k5here/counterexample)). Neither reports reach or state difference per mutant, and both search fresh seeds instead of scoring a fixed corpus. konserve's simulation document lists nine hand-chosen breaks under the heading "Does the suite actually bite?" ([konserve](https://github.com/replikativ/konserve/blob/main/doc/simulation-testing.md)).

For LLM agents the nearest work is Chronicle (arXiv, September 2026), which is record-replay, not seeded simulation. It generates all 192 first-order mutants of each guarded tool, kills 51, and classifies the 141 survivors: 110 "change code the recorded input never reaches, or change the guard without changing its decision on that input", and 31 "change output fields the assertion deliberately ignores" ([Chawla and Koul 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20625)). That is two of the thesis's bins merged into one, plus the oracle-tolerance bin, on six curated incidents with one recorded input each; the authors add that "the incidents, guards, and assertions were written together". AgentAssay (March 2026) defines mutation operators over prompts, tools, models and context under non-determinism; it was seen as a summary only and is a lead ([AgentAssay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02601)).

## Searches that came back empty, and how far to trust them

The following were looked for and not found. No peer-reviewed paper uses the term "deterministic simulation testing" together with mutation testing (four researchers ran overlapping queries; the hits were Antithesis and the repositories above). No source runs mutant and original on one seed of a DST harness and compares whole traces. No source runs an operator-generated mutant population against a DST harness or reports a mutation score or a verdict distribution for one; no DST project publishes a time-to-detect distribution for seeded bugs. No published operator set targets protocol-level logic in distributed implementations such as Raft or Paxos (quorum size, term comparison, persist-before-reply), Go channels or Rust async. No work compares fault-injection policies or workload generators for a distributed system against a mutant population. No source scores or minimises a simulator seed corpus by kill sets. No paper proposes deterministic execution or record-replay as the remedy for flaky mutation results in ordinary testing (two queries plus a keyword scan of Shi et al.). No source states, for a seeded simulator, whether same-seed comparison of mutant and original is sound. No software paper on ordinary application code was found that cites functional qualification or Certitude as its model, so the transfer from hardware to mainstream software testing is unestablished in either direction. No kill matrix was found for stateful, state-machine property tests, and none for linearizability or isolation checkers. One finding of the thesis's own spike, that an oracle reading a single channel carried in the simulated world goes blind when a mutant drops its own evidence, was not searched for at all. The nearest thing read is the Antithesis skill's anti-pattern of a mutant that "removes the logging or assertion that would have reported it", which it treats as a fault of the mutant.

These negatives deserve limited weight, for reasons the researchers recorded. **All searching went through Exa**; no general web search and no citation index was used, so "who cites PBMT" and "who cites Certitude" were answered by semantic search and are probably incomplete. Conference talks (Will Wilson's Strange Loop 2014 talk, BugBash) were not retrievable. Most negatives rest on one to three queries. And **Exa's machine summaries were wrong repeatedly**: a summary of Elle claimed "mutated implementations with seeded bugs" that the text does not contain; summaries of Mocket and of mu2 asserted bug injection and interleaving exploration that could not be confirmed; a summary of Voas's PIE paper invented content; summaries of several unrelated fuzzing papers falsely mentioned mutants; and Exa's publication dates were sometimes off by years (a 2009 article dated 2026). Claims that rest only on a summary are therefore leads: among them the four-class list attributed to da Silva et al.'s ISO 26262 slides, TraceLink's reported evaluation by injected mutations, Dinv's reported use of injected bugs against etcd Raft invariants, the "canary" mention in TigerBeetle's fuzz tests, and every negative about a DST project other than FoundationDB, ShardStore, Dropbox, TigerBeetle and Antithesis.

## What stays distinct, and what a paper must do differently

The defensible residue is a transfer and a measurement, not a method. The transfer: the hardware sort, applied where the stimulus is a seeded simulated world running production software, with the difference taken over the whole canonical trace. The measurement: how well that differential works as a classifier. Neither appears in any source read. A paper that claims more than this will be answered with Certitude, Reneri and the Antithesis post.

**Against Antithesis.** Antithesis owns per-property falsification on a DST platform, the baseline gate, the verdict ladder and the routing of fixes. A contribution has to differ in four ways the first-hand check supports. It must take the middle measurement by paired execution, original and mutant on the same seed with an automatic trace diff, where Antithesis infers divergence from an agent-placed marker and one randomized run. It must use mutants nobody chose, at population scale, and report a distribution of verdicts; Antithesis designs one realistic mutant per property and reports no score. It must answer Antithesis's objection to syntactic mutants with data, by reporting for an operator-generated population what share die by panic, what share by the intended invariant, and what share survive, which is Du et al.'s kill-reason split applied to DST. And it must score things Antithesis leaves unscored: members of a fixed seed corpus and competing generation policies. It should state that the skill's asset scripts and the hosted platform were not examined.

The full read of the skill removes two things from that list and adds two. Writing the prediction before the run, and counting a kill only when the targeted check fails for the predicted reason, are not differences: the skill does both, and its attribution rule is stricter than a single-run harness needs. What it adds: first, the skill's own stated limit, that it mutates only for properties that exist, is the gap a drawn mutant population addresses, and the paper should say so in the skill's words. Second, the thesis's source system has a finding the skill would discard. A dropped world successor carries away the interaction log the invariants read, so the mutant removes its own evidence without touching any check. The skill files that under mutants to redesign; the source system read it as a blind spot of an oracle with one channel, and answered with counts held against a second channel. That reading is a claim a paper can defend with data, and no source here makes it. A third, practical difference follows from the fixed corpus: re-running a fixed corpus of sixteen seeds costs under a minute per mutant in the source system's own measurements, against a search of tens of minutes per run, at the price of judging only against that corpus.

**Against hardware functional qualification.** The classification must be credited to that field and the claim limited to what the field lacks. Two things qualify. Certitude compares at declared observation points, and Brownell says the Non-Propagated / Non-Detected boundary is only that declaration; a whole-trace differential removes the choice, and the paper should show what that changes. And hardware gives no ratio between "missing tests" and "redundant code" for the Non-Propagated class. A paper that reruns its "equivalent on this corpus" survivors on fresh seeds and reports how many convert, against Schuler and Zeller's 75% precision and 56% recall and Görz et al.'s 11 equivalents in 100 stubborn mutants, would supply a number neither community has. The label itself needs a defence, since practitioners read that class as a probable weakness.

**Against the unit-test work on reach, infection, propagation and oracle.** Reneri and Ripples have the three measurements and the survivor classes; what they lack is a single baseline. The contribution is to show the cost difference concretely (one control run and exact equality against ten reruns and exclusion lists) and to defend the differential's soundness against the two threats this literature raises: a mutant that changes the number of random draws or the set of enabled events makes the same seed a different world, and a mutant can break the harness's determinism outright. Both need a guard and a measured rate. The paper must also say where its observation point sits, because Reneri and Ripples separate no-propagation from weak-oracle and the thesis's "differed / check fired" pair collapses them unless the trace compared is exactly what the invariants read. System-level results give it a prediction to test: pseudo-tested code and failed error propagation are both worse at system level than at unit level (both read as abstracts only: [Niedermayr et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2896941.2896944); [Jahangirova et al., Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wb15tdl7jdw)), so a DST harness should show a large reached-but-unchecked class.

**Against the two Raft repositories.** They establish that grading DST oracles with planted defects is in the air, on weak provenance. A paper differs by scale and by independence: operator-generated mutants in production code, not eight to twelve hand-planted defects; rules written before the mutants are seen, since the thesis's system so far has hand-written mutants and rules written from them, the same weakness Chronicle admits; a fixed corpus scored per member; and the reach and difference measurements neither repository takes. Hand-seeded faults are the least validated category of injected defect: Andrews et al. found operator mutants similar to real faults and hand-seeded ones different ([Andrews et al. 2005](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1062455.1062530), abstract only), so a coupling check against real past faults of the system matters more here than usual.

Four further points from the notes belong in any write-up. The complementary check is missing from the thesis: vacuity mutates the oracle, not the system, and Ball and Kupferman's definition for run-time monitors ("a test suite passes vacuously if it passes also with a monitor with fewer transitions") applies directly to invariants over traces ([Ball and Kupferman 2008](https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~ornak/publications/tap08.pdf)). An LLM used to judge equivalence for the "reached, no difference" class is unvalidated on a new codebase: a 2026 replication found LLM equivalence detectors degrade on additional datasets and names data leakage as a key factor ([Tandon et al. 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3832250), abstract only). Closing an oracle gap by asserting the observed value repeats a known failure of generated oracles, which "capture the actual program behaviour rather than the expected one" (abstract only) ([Konstantinou et al. 2024, Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/z13kg7gl8qz)). And a fixed mutant set used to tune a generator invites the overfitting that fuzzing benchmarks warn about.

## Conclusion

The search changed the shape of the claim. It began as "DST and mutation complete each other" and it ends as "DST is the setting in which a classification hardware has used since 2007 becomes cheap and exact for software, and nobody has measured it there". The first is a framing that Certitude users, Reneri's authors and Antithesis can each call their own; the second is an empirical question with no published answer in roughly 160 searches. The most valuable single number a paper could report is the confusion matrix of the differential used blind: how often "reached, no trace difference" is truly equivalent, and how often "difference, no finding" is a real oracle gap, on mutants nobody chose.

The timing matters as much as the content. Antithesis published eleven days before this review, the two Raft repositories appeared within the previous ten weeks, and Chronicle is a September 2026 preprint. The idea is arriving from several directions at once, and the window in which a same-seed differential on a DST harness is unpublished is short. The agent-loop subject is a distinct setting and a good one, but it is a venue for the measurement, not a contribution by itself. Before any novelty statement is made in public, three checks are cheap and outstanding: a citation-index pass on PBMT, Certitude and Reneri; a read of the Antithesis skill's scripts and a look at what its platform offers (its eight markdown files have now been read in full); and a full read of the sources this report could only label as leads.
