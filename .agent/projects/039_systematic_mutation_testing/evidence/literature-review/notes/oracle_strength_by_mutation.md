## 1. Oracle-quality measures: which exist, and which rest on mutation

### Takeaway
Mutation score is the reference "ground truth" against which every other oracle-quality metric (checked coverage, state coverage, state field coverage, coverage gap, oracle gap) is validated; the cheaper metrics are justified by correlation with it. Using mutants to rank or select what an oracle should check (Staats et al. 2012; OASIs 2016; SpecFuzzer) is established, so "mutants give a ground truth for the strength of each check" is old in unit-test and model-based settings.

### Cited Findings
- **Checked coverage** (Schuler & Zeller, ICST 2011; extended STVR 2013) [abstract, both]. Measures the share of covered statements in the dynamic backward slice of the oracle. "we introduce the concept of checked coverage -- the dynamic slice of covered statements that actually influence an oracle. Our experiments on seven open-source projects show that checked coverage is a sure indicator for oracle quality -- and even more sensitive than mutation testing, its much more demanding alternative." — [ICST 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICST.2011.32); [STVR 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/stvr.1497)
- **Pseudo-tested methods / extreme mutation** (Niedermayr, Juergens, Wagner, CSED@ICSE 2016) [abstract]. Removes whole method bodies; a covered method whose removal no test notices is pseudo-tested. "The results show that the ratio of pseudo-tested methods is acceptable for unit tests but not for system tests (that execute large portions of the whole system). Therefore, we conclude that the coverage metric is only a valid effectiveness indicator for unit tests." — [Niedermayr et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2896941.2896944). Note the direction: the larger the test (system level), the more code is reached without being checked.
- **Descartes replication** (Vera-Pérez, Danglot, Monperrus, Baudry, "A comprehensive study of pseudo-tested methods", EMSE 2019) [full, selectively]. "pseudo-tested methods exist in all our subjects"; "The ratio of pseudo-tested methods with respect to analyzed methods ranged from 1% to 46% in our dataset"; "pseudo-tested methods are significantly less tested than the other methods; yet, for most of them, the developers would not pay the testing price to fix this situation." — [arXiv 1807.05030](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.05030)
- **Oracle assessment and improvement / OASIs** (Jahangirova, Clark, Harman, Tonella, ISSTA 2016) [abstract, via Exa's copy; DOI not verified by fetch]. Oracle deficiencies are split into false positives (found by test generation) and false negatives (found by mutation): "Our technique combines test case generation to reveal false positives and mutation testing to reveal false negatives... the fault detection rate of the oracles after improvement increases, on average, by 48.6% (86% over the implicit oracle)." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/mfz8q1g02y0) (canonical: ISSTA 2016 proceedings). The OASIs tool paper (ISSTA 2018) and GAssert (Terragni et al.) are [snippet] only.
- **State Field Coverage** (Molina, Aguirre, Gorla, arXiv Oct 2025; an IEEE Xplore conference record exists, venue not verified) [full, selectively]. Static metric: "the proportion of an object's state, as statically defined by its class fields, that an oracle may access during test execution." Validated against mutation score on "273 representation invariants and 249,027 test assertions"; Pearson "coefficient of 0.54" for representation invariants and "∼0.45" for test assertions. Their critique of the existing approaches, mutation included: "These techniques typically rely on dynamic analyses that are computationally expensive"; and of mutation specifically: "Firstly, the result of the mutation analysis points to the mutants that are not detected by the oracle, but exploiting this feedback to improve the oracle itself (i.e., to recognize the relationship between mutants and improvements to the oracle) is non-trivial. Secondly, oracle assessment based on mutation usually combines test generation with mutation analysis, both computationally expensive tasks." — [arXiv 2510.03071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03071)
- **RIPR and test-oracle strategies** (Li & Offutt, TSE 2017; earlier ICST 2014) [abstract]. Twelve oracle strategies varying how much state is checked and how often, evaluated on tests generated from state machines: "(1) Test oracles must check more than runtime exceptions because checking exceptions alone is not effective at revealing failures. (2) Test oracles do not need to check the entire output state because checking partial states reveals nearly as many failures as checking entire states. (3) Test oracles do not need to check program states multiple times... checking state invariants is a reasonably effective low cost approach to creating test oracles." — [TSE 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2016.2597136)
- **Mutation used to choose what the oracle checks** (Staats, Gay, Heimdahl, ICSE 2012) [abstract, via Exa's copy]. "This approach is based on the use of mutation analysis to rank variables in terms of fault-finding effectiveness, thus automating the selection of the oracle data... fault finding improvements over current industrial best practice of up to 145.8% observed." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/9sd1631tpfy). Follow-ups (Gay et al. TSE 2015; Dodona, ISSTA 2014) are [snippet].
- **Coverage gap** (Hossain, Dwyer, Elbaum, et al., ICSE 2023) [abstract, via Exa's copy]. "we formally define the coverage gap in structural testing as the percentage of program structure that is executed but not observed by any test oracles... 13 Java applications, 16K test cases and 51.6K test assertions shows that even for mature test suites, the gap can be as high as 51 percentage points (pp) and 34pp on average... the coverage gap strongly and negatively correlates with a test suite's fault-detection effectiveness." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/54qlsr25zgb)
- **"Oracle gap" is an existing term** (Jain, Kalburgi, Le Goues, Groce, "Mind the Gap", ISSRE 2023 / arXiv Sept 2023) [full, selectively]. "We define the oracle gap as the difference between source code coverage and mutation score for a given software element." Its use: "to identify code (files or even functions) with unusually high coverage relative to mutation score"; they claim it "is more useful for this purpose than either metric alone, or alternatives like pseudotesting." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/gft8bs5ggy2). A 2026 comparison of three "oracle gap calculation approaches" (two checked-coverage variants and pseudo-tested statements) exists (Maton, Kapfhammer, McMinn) [abstract]. — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/3l9jrlygxsm)
- **Fault types missed at high coverage** (Schwartz, Puckett, Meng, Gay, JSS 2018) [abstract]. 45 fault types against high-coverage human-written suites: "there were specific types of faults found less frequently than others. However, improvements in the formulation and selection of test oracles could overcome these weaknesses." — [JSS 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2018.06.024)
- **Specification strength by mutation (verification setting)**. MutDafny (arXiv Nov 2025) [full, intro only]: "If a program with a mutant verifies, this may indicate a weakness in the specification"; empirical study "on 794 Dafny programs and a manual evaluation of 284 alive mutants." — [arXiv 2511.15403](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15403). An NFM 2020 paper on Lustre-style models reports each kill with the property that killed it (log lines of the form "KILLED at 7:7 ifelse by [Prop2]") and reads survivors as "a part of the model that is not covered by the properties." [full, selectively; authors not captured] — [NFM 2020 PDF](https://wdi.centralesupelec.fr/boulanger/publis/2020NFM.pdf). (Model-checking coverage proper is another researcher's scope.)
- **Mutants used to filter candidate invariants**: SpecFuzzer (Molina et al., ICSE 2022) [snippet] — per Exa's summary, invariants that kill no mutant are discarded and invariants are grouped by the set of mutants they kill. — [PDF](https://damorim.github.io/publications/Molina_ETAL_ICSE2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- The requester's term "oracle gap" collides with Jain et al.'s definition (coverage minus mutation score per code element). The claim's four-way sort is finer than that scalar, but the name and the idea "covered yet unkilled = weak oracle" are published.
- Niedermayr's result (pseudo-testedness is worse for system tests) predicts that a DST harness, which is a very large system test, will show a large reached-but-unchecked class; that is support for the claim's usefulness, not for its novelty.
- Per-property kill attribution exists in the verification setting (NFM 2020) and per-invariant kill sets exist for inferred invariants (SpecFuzzer); "mutants give each invariant a strength score" is therefore not new as an idea.

### Gaps
- Barr et al., "The oracle problem in software testing: a survey" (TSE 2015) and Papadakis et al., "Mutation testing advances" were not read (title/summary only); no claim is taken from them.
- State coverage (Koster & Kao; Vanoverberghe et al.) and Zhang & Mesbah's assertion-count study were seen only as [snippet].

## 2. "Killed by any failure" versus "killed by the intended assertion or property"

### Takeaway
The distinction is established and measured: Du, Palepu & Jones (ISSTA 2023) show crashes account for up to 43.8% of killed mutants, Major records the kill reason per mutant, and Bartocci et al.'s PBMT makes the property-specific kill a definition. Mainstream tools otherwise define a kill as "any test fails" and most count timeouts as detected.

### Cited Findings
- **Du, Palepu, Jones, "To Kill a Mutant: An Empirical Study of Mutation Testing Kills", ISSTA 2023** [full, selectively]. Taxonomy: "We classify test-case failures encountered during mutation testing into three main categories: (1) test oracles, (2) source-code oracles, and (3) crashes." Result on 10 Java projects with a modified PIT: "crashes can cause as much as 46.2% of failed test runs and as much as 43.8% of killed mutants (and thus the mutation score)... test oracles (which are the traditionally assumed cause of mutation kills) contributed as little as 11.8% of test failures for one subject." They state the novelty: "in our literature review we find no prior study addressing the notion that the same mutant can be killed in different ways (crash vs. oracles) by multiple tests." — [ISSTA 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597926.3598090)
- **Li & Offutt** (above): the "null oracle" that only observes exceptions "is not effective at revealing failures" — the same distinction made from the oracle side. — [TSE 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2016.2597136)
- **Major** [full, manual]: exports "killed.csv: The reason why a mutant was killed — i.e., assertion failure, exception, or timeout", and prints the split in its summary line ("Mutants killed / live: 76 (76-0-0) / 10"); it also reports "Mutants covered". — [Major manual](https://mutation-testing.org/doc/major.pdf)
- **PIT** [full, concepts page]: outcomes are "Killed - Survived - No coverage - Non viable - Timed Out - Memory error - Run error"; "Killed means a test caught the mutation successfully... No coverage is the same as Survived except there were no tests that exercised the line of code where the mutation was created." No distinction by failure cause. — [pitest.org](https://pitest.org/quickstart/basic_concepts/)
- **Stryker** [full, states page]: "Killed: When at least one test failed while this mutant was active"; Timeout "is counted as 'detected'. The logic here is that if this mutant were to be injected in your code, your CI build would detect it because the tests will never complete". Its metrics are defined as formulas: Detected is `killed + timeout`, Undetected is `survived + no coverage`, Covered is `detected + survived`, and the mutation score is `detected / valid * 100`. — [stryker-mutator.io](https://stryker-mutator.io/docs/mutation-testing-elements/mutant-states-and-metrics/)
- **cargo-mutants** [full, two pages]: exit codes separate the cases: "0: Success! Every viable mutant that was tested was caught by a test... 2: Found some mutants that were not covered by tests. 3: Some tests timed out: possibly the mutations caused an infinite loop, or the timeout is too low." — [mutants.rs exit codes](https://mutants.rs/exit-codes.html); default "test timeout is 5 times the baseline test time, with a minimum of 20 seconds" — [mutants.rs timeouts](https://mutants.rs/timeouts.html)
- **Pitest timeouts and JVM crashes** (Laurent, Wall, Ventresque, 2020) [abstract, via Exa's copy]: "When a mutant leads to a JVM crash, because of a timeout or a memory error for example, Pitest does not record the full information about a mutant, leading to an incomplete matrix"; the missing data "has little impact on the computed set of minimal mutants." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/v3p38t2lv4p)
- **PBMT follow-up work**: the searches surfaced only the authors' own material — Drishti Yadav's 2024 thesis "From Fault Injection to Formal Verification: A Holistic Approach to Fault Diagnosis in Cyber-Physical Systems" and the FIM Simulink tool [both snippet]. — [thesis, Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/3bqk9r6mhnm); [FIM PDF](https://www.tuwien.at/doc/res/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/fim-fault-injection-and-mutation-for-simulink.pdf)
- **Practitioner statements of the same distinction in DST** (see section 6): the dst-harness README reports a mutant caught by an unhandled `IndexError` "as `crash` rather than folded into the invariant column. 'It threw' is a symptom; the invariants give a diagnosis, and conflating the two flatters the harness." — [dst-harness](https://github.com/asp53826/dst-harness). Antithesis: "simple syntactic changes are not good mutations because they mostly lead to run-time panics and short-circuit the validation of distributed safety properties." — [Antithesis blog, 2026-09-25](https://antithesis.com/blog/2026/mutation-testing/)

### Inferences
- A harness that reports "killed by invariant X" versus "killed by crash/timeout" is applying Du et al.'s taxonomy plus PBMT's definition; neither part is new. What tools lack is the per-check attribution as a default output (only Major records the reason, and only as assertion/exception/timeout).
- Antithesis's remark is a direct warning for source-level operator mutation inside DST: many generic mutants die by panic before any invariant is exercised, so an unqualified mutation score would overstate invariant strength.

### Gaps
- mutmut's status list could not be sourced: the retrieved docs page (current mutmut) describes timeout configuration ("(duration_of_original_tests + timeout_constant) * timeout_multiplier") but not the outcome categories. — [mutmut docs](https://mutmut.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
- No peer-reviewed work extending PBMT beyond its authors was found (two queries); this is absence in my searches, not proof of absence.
- Kim, An, Feldt, Yoo (SIMFL) reportedly separate kill reasons (assertion, exception, timeout) for fault localisation; seen only as [snippet].

## 3. Automatic classification of live mutants by reach / infection / propagation / reveal, by comparing original and mutant executions

### Takeaway
This exists as working tools with published numbers: Reneri (2019) sorts undetected extreme mutants into no-infection / no-propagation / weak-oracle by diffing recorded states of original and mutant runs, and Du et al. (ICSE 2024) instrument four RIPR checkpoints over 1.1 million mutated test runs. Both must rerun the original about 10 times to filter nondeterministic state — the cost a deterministic harness removes.

### Cited Findings
- **Theory**: Voas's PIE (TSE 1992) [abstract]: "statistically estimating three program characteristics...: (1) the probability that a particular section of a program is executed, (2) the probability that the particular section affects the data state, and (3) the probability that a data state produced by that section has an effect on program output." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/3lgkgrh48kq). Woodward & Halewood (1988) [abstract]: "strong mutation testing and weak mutation testing are in fact extreme ends of a spectrum... The term firm mutation is introduced to represent the middle ground", with "greater user control over the nature of result comparison." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/98v6qzdl66d)
- **Reneri** (Vera-Pérez, Danglot, Monperrus, Baudry, arXiv 2019) [full]. Method: record state at the mutated method's exit and in the test code, for original and transformed runs, and diff. Classes: "no-infection (ni): there is no observable difference in the local state of the program after the method invocation...; no-propagation (np): there is an observable difference in the program state at the transformation point, but there is no observable difference in the state of the test case; weak-oracle (wo): the program state infection is propagated to the code of a test case but no assertion fails." Numbers on 15 projects, 312 undetected extreme transformations: "198 out of the 312 undetected extreme transformations. That is, in 63% of the cases, the existing test inputs do trigger an infection of the local program state at the transformation point. The rest 114 (37%) does not provoke a program infection"; "Answer to RQ2: 42% of the transformations infecting the program state, propagate to an observable point." Nondeterminism handling: "we execute the tests N times when recording the states of the original methods and another N times when recorded their transformed variants... default value of 10"; "Only the state elements that remained unchanged are kept for the state comparison." — [arXiv 1909.04770](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04770)
- **Ripples of a Mutation** (Du, Palepu, Jones, ICSE 2024) [full, selectively]. "For every mutated test run, we are able to determine: (a) if the mutation induced a state infection; (b) if the infection propagated through the end of the test run; and (c) if the test failed in the presence of a propagated infection" over "more than 43,000 mutations executed across 1.1 million test runs." Numbers: "infection rates ranging from 64.3% to 94.1%, and propagation rates ranging from 84.9% to 92.6%, given infection"; "approximately 18.5%-89.4% of surviving mutants could potentially be killed based on infection or propagation information." On the last stage: "there is a noticeable disparity between propagation and test revealing. Moreover, infection usually exhibits as method exit anomalies, such as uncaught exceptions, thrown within the mutated method and leads to early termination of mutation test runs, leaving test oracles unchecked." Definition: "we define 'infection' as any differences found between the mutated and original non-mutated program run in the memory sta[te]..." at method exit. Cost of nondeterminism: "The mutated run is performed first, followed by several rounds (10 times in our experiment) of the original test runs. The multiple rounds... help guard against mistakenly treating non-deterministic runtime state as possible infection, and flaky execution flows"; tests with flaky flows are excluded and "an exclusion list of fields that store non-deterministic states" is built. — [ICSE 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597503.3639179)
- **Crossfire** (Du, Palepu, Jones, arXiv Nov 2024) [full, selectively]. "a fine-grained memory-state analysis reveals that 19%-84% of surviving mutants exhibit propagated infections not revealed by test cases, which can be killed through assertion augmentation of existing tests." Again: "we performed N repetitive test runs on the original program to identify nondeterministic memory locations." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/5v4yjg3sv9s)
- **Just, Ernst, Fraser, ISSTA 2014** (Major) [full, selectively]. Runtime infection conditions evaluated on the unmutated run: "state infection conditions can reveal — with a single test execution of the unmutated program — which mutants would lead to a different state." "The state infection optimization identifies 3–42% of covered mutants as test-equivalent for every test in the test suite"; overall "reduced the mutation analysis time by 40% on average" on 14 programs. Used for speed, not for diagnosing survivors. — [PDF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/state-infection-issta2014.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2610384.2610388)
- **Impact-based equivalence judgement (Javalanche)**. Schuler, Dallmeier, Zeller, ISSTA 2009 [full, abstract/intro]: "mutations that violate invariants are significantly more likely to be detectable by a test suite." — [PDF](https://www.st.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/files/schuler-issta-2009.pdf). Schuler & Zeller, STVR 2013 [abstract + intro]: "If a mutant changes the coverage of a run, it is more likely to be non-equivalent"; on 140 manually classified mutations of seven Java programs: "(i) the problem is serious and widespread—about 45% of all undetected mutants turned out to be equivalent; (ii) manual classification takes time—about 15 min per mutation; (iii) coverage is a simple, efficient and effective means to identify equivalent mutants—with a classification precision of 75% and a recall of 56%; and (iv) coverage as an equivalence detector is superior to the state of the art, in particular violations of dynamic invariants." — [PDF](https://www.st.cs.uni-saarland.de/publications/files/schuler-stvrbis-2013.pdf)
- **Failed error propagation** (Jahangirova, Clark, Harman, Tonella, 2019/2020) [abstract]. On Defects4J (386 real bugs): "the prevalence of failed error propagation is negligible when testing is performed at the unit level. However, when system-level inputs are provided, the prevalence of failed error propagation increases substantially... intermediate steps should be checked when testing at system level." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wb15tdl7jdw)
- **Coverage-only classification in mainstream tools**: PIT "No coverage", Stryker "No coverage" and Major "Mutants covered" give the reached / not-reached split by default (links in section 2).
- **State-comparison used only for speed** [snippet]: AccMut ("equivalence modulo states", Wang et al. 2017), WinMut (2021), "Mutation Analysis with Execution Taints" (Gopinath & Görz 2024). — [AccMut, Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/b8ptghflnn0)

### Inferences
- The claim's part (b) maps onto existing categories: "world gap" = not reached (PIT/Stryker "no coverage"); "equivalent on this corpus" = Reneri's no-infection / Just et al.'s "test-equivalent" mutant; "oracle gap" = Reneri's weak-oracle / Du et al.'s "propagated but not revealed". The three-measurement sort is therefore published for Java unit tests since 2019.
- What the literature does not have, in what I found: (i) the comparison made on a whole-run canonical trace of a deterministic simulation, where one baseline run suffices and any difference is attributable to the mutant; (ii) Reneri and Du et al. distinguish no-propagation from weak-oracle, a split the claim's "ran differed / check fired" pair collapses unless the trace is the thing the invariants read.
- The nearest prior numbers for the claim's classes: 37% no-infection, about 37% no-propagation, about 27% weak-oracle (Reneri, extreme mutants; derived from 114/312, and 42% of 198 ≈ 83 weak-oracle, ≈ 115 no-propagation — the last two counts are my arithmetic, not quoted); 19–84% of survivors "propagated but not revealed" (Crossfire, conventional mutants).
- Schuler & Zeller's coverage-impact heuristic is the direct ancestor of "run differed from baseline implies non-equivalent"; their 75% precision / 56% recall shows trace difference is a useful but imperfect equivalence signal at the coverage level.

### Gaps
- No quantitative weak-versus-strong kill-rate comparison was read: Offutt & Lee, "How strong is weak mutation?" (1991) was seen only as a Semantic Scholar TLDR [snippet].
- Chekam et al. on stubborn mutants, Yao/Harman/Jia on equivalent and stubborn mutants, and predictive mutation testing were not read.
- Just et al.'s RQ2 figure (infected without propagating inside the enclosing expression) was not extracted cleanly from the PDF text.

## 4. Flaky tests and nondeterminism as a threat to mutation testing

### Takeaway
The effect is measured and material (Shi et al.: 9% of mutant-test pairs unknown, mutation scores moving by four points between runs; Alshammari et al.: 19% of mutants killed by flaky tests are killed because of flakiness), and mutants themselves induce flakiness (ICSE 2026). The remedies proposed are rerunning, isolation and de-duplication; none of the papers read proposes deterministic execution as the remedy.

### Cited Findings
- **Shi, Bell, Marinov, ISSTA 2019** [full, selectively]. "Mutation testing implicitly assumes that tests exhibit deterministic behavior, in terms of their coverage and the outcome of a test (not) killing a certain mutant." "in our experiments, mutation scores vary by four percentage points on average between repeated executions, and 9% of mutant-test pairs have an unknown status"; "about 4% of mutants are unknown"; coverage is nondeterministic even with no failing test ("We observe no test failures in any of the runs... However, we find many statements are non deterministically covered — in the case of cloudera.oryx, 74%..."). Remedy: "(1) improve coverage collection..., (2) force reruns of tests on mutants not covered..., and (3) prioritize tests"; "Our techniques reduce the number of 'unknown' (flaky) mutants by 79.4%." They also say "Non-deterministic coverage is not necessarily bad; such non-determinism may even be intended or hard to control." — [ISSTA 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3293882.3330568)
- **Alshammari, Ammann, Hilton, Bell, 2024** [abstract]. "Our analysis of 22 Java projects... reveals that 19% of mutants killed by these flaky tests result from the flakiness introduced by at least one test"; failure de-duplication "approximates the true number of mutants reliably killed, but with far less computational cost than re-running each test for each mutant." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wbvgmfx376r)
- **FlakiMe** (Cordy, Rwemalika, Franci, Papadakis, Harman, ICSE 2022) [abstract]. "a 5% of flakiness failures is enough to affect the mutation score, but the effect size is modest (2% - 4%), while it completely annihilates the ability of program repair to patch 50% of the subject programs." — [arXiv 1912.03197](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03197)
- **Test Flimsiness** (ICSE 2026; authors not captured, PDF hosted on Owain Parry's page) [abstract]. "Previous studies quantified the impact of pre-existing flaky tests on mutation testing, but we reveal that mutations themselves can induce flakiness... calling into question the reliability of any technique that relies on deterministic test outcomes in response to mutations... over half a million test suite executions across 28 Python projects... over 30 million mutant-test pairs unveiled flimsiness in 54% of projects." — [ICSE 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744916.3773125); [PDF](https://o-parry.github.io/papers/2026a.pdf)
- **Nondeterminism handled by repetition in the trace-diff work**: Reneri (N = 10 runs each side) and Ripples (10 original runs, exclusion lists) — quotes in section 3.
- **Determinism treated as a checked property in DST practice**: dst-harness "runs the same seed in two subprocesses under different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values and compares digests." — [dst-harness](https://github.com/asp53826/dst-harness)

### Inferences
- "Determinism gives mutation clean measurements" is not stated in the mutation literature I read; that literature treats nondeterminism as something to be averaged out. The complementarity argument in claim (b) is therefore not contradicted and not pre-empted by these papers — but see section 6 for a DST vendor doing the measurement.
- "Test Flimsiness" is a caution for the claim: a mutant can make a run nondeterministic (for example by changing iteration order or timing), so "same seed, different trace" needs a guard that the mutant has not broken the harness's determinism itself.

### Gaps
- Whether deterministic replay or record/replay has been proposed specifically as a remedy for mutation flakiness: not found in two queries (q08, q18); a "deterministic|record and replay" keyword scan of Shi et al. found no such proposal.
- Fellner et al., "Mutation Testing with Hyperproperties" (potentially vs definitely killed for nondeterministic models) seen as [snippet] only.

## 5. The LLM-era strand

### Takeaway
LLMs are now used as mutant generators (more bug-like mutants, but more equivalents and duplicates) and as equivalence judges (promising results that a 2026 replication attributes partly to data leakage), and several papers warn that generated oracles pin actual rather than intended behaviour.

### Cited Findings
- **Generators**: Wang et al., "A Comprehensive Study on Large Language Models for Mutation Testing" (2024) [abstract]: on 851 real bugs, LLM mutants reach "87.98% (for LLMs) vs. 41.64% (for rule-based)" fault detection, but "have worse non-compilability, duplication, and equivalent mutant rates by 26.60, 10.14, and 3.51 percentage points." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/zmr6glzdcss)
- **Equivalence judges**: Tian et al., ISSTA 2024 [abstract]: on "3,302 method-level Java mutant pairs", "LLM-based techniques significantly outperform existing techniques (i.e., the average improvement of 35.69% in terms of F1-score)." — [ISSTA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3650212.3680395). Counter-evidence, Tandon et al. (ACM, 2026; title not captured) [abstract]: "Contrary to prior findings, the proposed LLM-based EMD techniques suffer substantial performance degradation on the two additional datasets... a key factor... original-method–level data leakage." — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3832250)
- **Tests that pin current behaviour**: Konstantinou, Degiovanni, Papadakis (2024) [abstract]: "LLM-based test generation approaches are also prone on generating oracles that capture the actual program behaviour rather than the expected one." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/z13kg7gl8qz). Zhao, Zhou, Cohen (2026) [abstract]: "buggy code steers LLMs toward generating tests that validate its erroneous behavior rather than expose it." — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wscv904x3wg)

- **Agent-written mutants against DST properties**: Antithesis, section 6.

### Inferences
- An LLM used as the equivalence judge for the "ran, no trace difference" class should be treated as unvalidated on a new codebase, given the 2026 replication.
- Closing an "oracle gap" by adding an assertion on the observed value is exactly the actual-versus-expected trap these papers describe.

## 6. Direct prior art for the claim itself (DST harness + mutation)

### Takeaway
Part (a) and most of part (b) have been done in public within the last ten weeks: Antithesis (2026-09-25) runs agent-written mutants against a deterministic fault-injection harness and reports each attempt as "caught / buggy code never ran / bug ran, nothing noticed / no usable result", per safety property; a small open-source harness (2026-07-31) scores its oracles by seeds-to-first-catch on planted Raft bugs. Neither reports a baseline-trace diff per mutant, and neither uses systematic source-level operators.

### Cited Findings
- **Antithesis, "Why we taught agents to break distributed safety properties"** (Rohan Padhye, 2026-09-25) [full text of the post; the interactive per-attempt details were not readable]. Purpose: "a new agent skill to perform mutation testing, injecting artificial bugs to validate or refine Antithesis tests until properties are falsifiable (i.e., the test should fail when a property-violating bug is present)" — framed as "the classic test oracle problem." Outcome legend, per property and attempt: "Artificial bug was caught / Buggy code never ran / Bug ran, nothing noticed / No usable result." Responses recorded per class include "Added node stops to the fault mix", "New driver: hold the WAL open", "Doubled the run length", "Added a check inside rqlite", "Redesigned the bug". Scale: "After 19 mutant injections across 46 runs of 15-60 minutes each (~24 hours cumulative fuzzing duration)... 11 of 13 safety properties were successfully falsified"; "our Antithesis tests uncovered three new upstream rqlite bugs." Mutants are property-targeted and agent-written, not operator-generated: "simple syntactic changes are not good mutations because they mostly lead to run-time panics." — [Antithesis blog](https://antithesis.com/blog/2026/mutation-testing/)
- **asp53826/dst-harness** (GitHub, created 2026-07-31, 0 stars; personal project, low authority) [full README]. "Every run is a pure function of its seed." "Twelve defects, each one a mistake that has been made in a real Raft, planted one at a time. The number that matters is... how many seeds it took" — table of mutant / first failing seed (1 to 485) / "caught by" (named invariant, linearizability, or crash). "`kv_no_dedup` and `local_read` are caught only by the linearizability checker, and never by any Raft invariant." A surviving mutant exposed a generator defect: "`single_slot` survived 400 seeds... five torn writes in sixty runs. Crashes drawn uniformly over a six-second run essentially never land inside a 4ms fsync window, so a fault that existed in the model was, in practice, unreachable"; "'not caught'... would be a statement about the nemesis and not about the code." — [dst-harness](https://github.com/asp53826/dst-harness)
- **Older, adjacent**: Dinv (Grant, Cech, Beschastnikh, ICSE 2018) [abstract, truncated] asserts inferred etcd Raft invariants at runtime "using these asserts to det[ect]..." — Exa's summary says injected bugs; not verified. — [Exa copy](https://exa.ai/library/publication/hxlbxykm3z6). Schuler et al. 2009 (section 3) use dynamic invariants as the mutant-impact detector. PBMT (already read by the requester) is the academic statement of property-relative kills on simulated CPS models.

### Inferences
- Claim (a) is old as of 2026: invariant strength (Antithesis per-property falsification; dst-harness "caught by"), workload/fault-generator quality (Antithesis "changed which faults get injected"; dst-harness's unreachable torn-write window) and the reach-versus-check split are all demonstrated on DST harnesses. "Value of each corpus member" was not seen: both systems search fresh seeds rather than score a fixed seed corpus (dst-harness's seeds-to-first-catch is the closest).
- Claim (b)'s residue, in what I found: the middle measurement (did the mutant's trace differ from the unmutated trace on the same seed) and hence the "equivalent on this corpus" class; systematic source-level operators with kill-reason attribution; and the explicit argument that determinism replaces the 10x baseline reruns that Reneri and Du et al. need. Antithesis's three informative classes are caught / not reached / reached-but-unnoticed, with no trace-difference class.
- Antithesis's "No usable result" class and its "Independence test rejected it" note show that even on a deterministic platform a fourth, inconclusive outcome was needed.

### Gaps
