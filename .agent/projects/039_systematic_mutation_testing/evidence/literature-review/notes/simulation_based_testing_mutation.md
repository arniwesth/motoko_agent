# Mutation testing combined with simulation-based testing (CPS, autonomous driving, robotics, discrete-event simulation) and the lineage of "Property-Based Mutation Testing"

- Reading depth is tagged: **[full]** = I read the full text (or the first 90k characters) and the quote is verbatim; **[abstract]** = abstract only, quote verbatim; **[summary]** = only Exa's machine-written summary was read, so the statement is unverified and must not be quoted as the paper's words.
- Raw Exa responses are in `/workspaces/motoko_agent/tmp/research/exa_raw/simulation_based_testing_mutation/` (`q01`..`q25` searches, `c01`..`c05` full-text fetches).
- "Exa copy" means I read Exa's library copy and could not confirm a DOI/arXiv ID inside my search budget; the title, authors and venue are as Exa lists them.
- Bottom line for the claim under review: each half has published prior art in this field. Half (a) (mutants as ground truth for scenarios, generators and oracles) is established practice. Half (b) (sorting survivors by where they stopped) exists as a three-way split in DaMAT (2023) and as a weak/strong/equivalent split in a 2026 vision paper for ADS. I found no paper that combines all three per-mutant measurements (reached, trace differs, check fired) on a seeded deterministic simulation; the field mostly treats simulators as nondeterministic and pays for that with repeated runs and statistics.

## 1. What cites or extends "Property-Based Mutation Testing" (Bartocci, Mariani, Nickovic, Yadav, ICST 2023)?

### Takeaway
The follow-up is small and mostly from the same group or from Nejati's group: a thesis and doctoral-symposium paper (Yadav), a neural mutant generator evaluated under both kill notions (BERTiMuS, 2025), a fault-injection reflection paper (2025), an industrial Simulink report (ASE 2026), one transfer outside CPS (RESTestBench, 2026), and one critique-plus-extension for autonomous driving (Shin, 2026). Citing papers accept the "kill = property violated on the mutant but not on the original" definition as correct and criticise scope (monolithic Simulink models, no hazard-derived faults, no closed loop, one property at a time); none reports higher-order PBMT or a public PBMT tool beyond FIM.

### Cited Findings
- BERTiMuS restates the PBMT kill and adopts it beside the classical one: "Bartocci et al. [4] propose a new notion of mutant killing, asserting that a test case kills a mutant if executing the test case on the mutant leads to a requirement violation, whereas executing the same test case on the original code does not result in such a violation. This contrasts with the classical notion of mutant killing, where a mutant is considered killed by a test case if the outputs from executing the test case on the mutant and on the original code differ." **[full]** — [Zhang, Ghobari, Sabetzadeh, Nejati, "Simulink Mutation Testing using CodeBERT", arXiv 2501.07553, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07553)
- BERTiMuS states the ordering of the two notions: "The requirements-aware notion is stronger than the classical one: If a test case kills a mutant under the requirements-aware notion, it also does so under the classical notion, but not vice versa." It generated 387 compilable mutants against 594 from FIM on five industrial-benchmark models, and "under the requirements-aware notion of mutation testing, BERTiMuS significantly outperforms FIM." **[full]** — [arXiv 2501.07553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07553)
- Shin (2026) on what PBMT got right and wrong: "Targeting Simulink data-flow models, PBMT effectively filters out semantically irrelevant faults by anchoring kill criteria to explicit temporal specifications. However, its scope remains confined to internal block or signal perturbations within monolithic architectures, overlooking emergent hazards arising from degraded inter-module communication in modern modular systems. Furthermore, PBMT does not systematically derive fault injections from formal hazard analyses, nor does it account for closed-loop control dynamics." **[full]** — [Shin, "Towards Safety-Aware Mutation Testing for Autonomous Driving Systems", arXiv 2606.26456, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)
- RESTestBench carries PBMT outside CPS to REST API tests: "Bartocci et al. [12] introduce Property-Based Mutation Testing (PBMT), emphasizing that mutants are only meaningful when tied to a specific property"; the benchmark "assesses test adequacy using specific mutations relevant to the requirement, drawing inspiration from Bartocci et al. and their PBMT definition", and calls PBMT "a more challenging metric than normal mutation testing". **[full, PBMT passages only]** — [Kogler et al., "RESTestBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Effectiveness of LLM-Generated REST API Test Cases from NL Requirements", 2026 (Exa copy; canonical not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/vpxphz5mpf6)
- Yadav, Mandrioli, Bartocci, Bianculli (2025) list tool gaps for Simulink fault injection and mutation; one is equivalent mutants from lack of semantic awareness: "Almost all tools lack semantic awareness. The only exception is BERTiMuS [16] ... Without semantic awareness, injecting some type of faults into specific parts of the model can lead to the creation of 'equivalent mutants' (borrowing the terminology from MT) that do not affect the behavior of the system, making the fault injection ineffective." Also: "Only two tools support FI for Stateflow charts, and no tool supports LUT fault injection." **[full, partial read]** — [Yadav et al., "Fault Injection for Simulink-based CPS Models: Insights and Future Directions", 2025 (Exa copy; canonical not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/szvpzz2ql2r)
- Industrial use of Simulink mutation testing (not PBMT itself, but the Exa summary says the paper advocates the property-based view; I read only the abstract): "During a pilot project with our tool prototype MUT4SLX we learned that equivalent mutants, requirement traceability, and mutation testing for Stateflow represent particular challenges not yet reported in the academic literature." Company: DUCO (building ventilation). **[abstract]** — [Kavak et al., "Mutation Testing of Simulink Cyber-Physical System Models: Challenges and Solutions in Practice", ASE 2026, arXiv 2608.24250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24250)
- Yadav's thesis reuses the PBMT definitions verbatim (first-order vs higher-order mutant, killed, live, equivalent, invalid) and defines mutation coverage as "#killed mutants / (#valid mutants − #equivalent mutants)". The only occurrence of "higher order" in the first 120k characters is the background definition; I found no higher-order PBMT experiment. **[full, first 120k chars]** — [Yadav, "Enhancing Fault Diagnosis in Safety-Critical Cyber-Physical Systems", PhD thesis, TU Wien, DOI 10.34726/hss.2025.125790](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/209539/1/Yadav%20Drishti%20-%202024%20-%20Enhancing%20fault%20diagnosis%20in%20safety-critical...pdf)
- Yadav's ISSTA 2024 doctoral-symposium paper presents PBMT as one part of a fault-injection-to-verification pipeline. **[summary]** — [Yadav, "From Fault Injection to Formal Verification: A Holistic Approach to Fault Diagnosis in Cyber-Physical Systems", ISSTA 2024, DOI 10.1145/3650212.3685552](https://doi.org/10.1145/3650212.3685552)
- The same four authors' later paper on signal feature coverage (TOSEM 2025) uses mutation-based evaluation of Simulink test suites; the Exa summary says PBMT is used to evaluate test suites there. **[summary]** — [Bartocci, Mariani, Ničković, Yadav, "Signal Feature Coverage and Testing for CPS Dataflow Models", ACM TOSEM 2025](https://boa.unimib.it/retrieve/252d0f14-dc07-4fd5-b284-5113bcd701ae/Bartocci%20et%20al-2025-ACM%20Transactions%20on%20Software%20Engineering%20and%20Methodology-VoR.pdf)
- A 2026 preprint on metamorphic-relation adequacy treats property-aware mutation as a precursor and criticises it for targeting one formal requirement at a time. **[summary]** — ["A semantic mutation metric for metamorphic relation adequacy ...", arXiv 2605.17437](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17437v2)

### Inferences
- PBMT's lasting contribution in the citing literature is the two-level kill (output differs vs. property violated). BERTiMuS's "stronger than" statement is exactly the relation the claim under review uses between "run differed" and "check fired".
- Nobody citing PBMT adds a reachability measurement or classifies survivors; the citing work measures mutation scores under one or two kill notions.

### Gaps
- No citation index was available through Exa; the list comes from semantic search (queries q01, q02, q23) and is probably incomplete. A Google Scholar or Semantic Scholar "cited by" pass would close this.
- No higher-order-mutant PBMT follow-up and no port to another modelling language (SCADE, Modelica, AADL) was found. Absence in three searches is weak evidence.
- I did not read the full Kavak et al. (ASE 2026) paper; its treatment of requirement traceability may be the closest industrial test of PBMT.

## 2. Mutation in simulation-based testing of ADS, robotics and other CPS: what is mutated and how is a kill decided?

### Takeaway
Three different things are called "mutation" here. (1) Mutating the model or controller (Simulink blocks, planner weights, C/C++ flight or satellite code, messages between modules) with a kill decided by output difference, by a trajectory/safety metric, or by a property violation. (2) Mutating data exchanged between components (DaMAT, SAMT). (3) Mutating the scenario, which is fuzzing/search and has no kill at all. Using mutants of the system as ground truth to grade scenario suites, test selectors and test generators is long-standing practice (Simulink since 2005; ADAS scenario sampling 2022; Apollo 2022; ArduPilot 2021; DRL agents 2024).

### Cited Findings
- ADS, planner parameters, deterministic simulator (closest to the claim's "value of each scenario"): "we propose a mutation-based approach that mutates the weights of the cost function and then checks if at least one scenario of T kills the mutant. Preliminary experiments on a manually designed test suite show that some weights are easier to cover ... and that more complicated scenarios (that generate more complex paths) are those that allow to cover more weights." **[abstract]** — [Laurent, Arcaini, Ishikawa, Ventresque, "A Mutation-Based Approach for Assessing Weight Coverage of a Path Planner", APSEC 2019 (Exa copy; DOI not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/nrk343sh4vh)
- The journal extension defines coverage of a parameter by a scenario as "run differs from the unmutated run": "A parameter is covered by a scenario if changing the parameter's value leads to a significantly different behaviour of the ADS in this scenario", and says the earlier version was for "the simpler context of an ADS with a deterministic simulation". Kill metrics: the ego path, safety ("minimum Euclidean distance to the other cars") and comfort ("maximum acceleration"). Subject: Autonomoose, 46 parameters of the local and behavioural planners. **[full, first 90k chars]** — [Laurent, Klikovits, Arcaini, Ishikawa, Ventresque, "Parameter Coverage for Testing of Autonomous Driving Systems under Uncertainty", ACM TOSEM 2022 (Exa copy; DOI believed 10.1145/3550270, not confirmed)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/g2911qsmmwz)
- ADS, messages between modules (vision paper, no experiments): "A key innovation in our vision is the shift from mutating each component (e.g., the planning module's source code) to mutating the messages exchanged between ADS modules." Mutants run in CARLA; "execution can be stopped as soon as a mutant is definitively killed". **[full]** — [Shin, arXiv 2606.26456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)
- ADS, source-code mutants of Apollo used to grade test reduction and prioritisation: "We further developed a mutation testing tool to systematically inject errors to different modules of Apollo and evaluated the effectiveness of reduced and prioritized ADS tests ... The reduced driving recordings have comparable test effectiveness—they detected 99% of injected faults that were detected by the original driving recordings". Tests are replayed recordings from the SVL simulator. **[full, partial read; the detection criterion was not located]** — [Deng, Zheng, Zhang, Lou, Zhang, "Scenario-Based Test Reduction and Prioritization for Multi-Module Autonomous Driving Systems", arXiv preprint dated 2022-09-04 per Exa (venue believed ESEC/FSE 2022; arXiv ID and DOI not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/4x5ydq3ggrt)
- ADAS, mutants used to grade scenario samplers: "we leverage variability modelling techniques to select scenarios from a scenario space and assess the resulting scenario suites with a mutation score as metric ... sampling based on interaction coverage reduces the testing effort significantly while maintaining effectiveness in terms of mutation scores." Two AEB implementations on an industrial simulator. **[abstract]** — [Birkemeyer, Pett, Vogelsang, Seidl, Schaefer, "Feature-Interaction Sampling for Scenario-based Testing of Advanced Driver Assistance Systems", VaMoS 2022 (Exa copy; DOI not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/wqymrcg7tc3)
- Robotics (ArduPilot in software-in-the-loop simulation), mutation score as ground truth for input quality: "using the mutation score [76] as the ground-truth measure for the effectiveness of test inputs [141, 222], I evaluated different coverage-based test input quality metrics. I observed high levels of non-determinism among test executions, which indicates the necessity to execute test inputs multiple times and look at the outcome of the test executions collectively ... most coverage metrics are poor indicators of fault finding effectiveness ... the union coverage, the collection of all lines or branches executed over multiple runs of the same test, has a higher correlation with the mutation score". Kill is output difference: "measuring the number of these executions that result in different outputs than running the same test suite on the original, unmutated program." **[full for the introduction and background; the evaluation chapter lies beyond the 90k characters fetched]** — [Afzal, "Automated Testing of Robotic and Cyberphysical Systems", PhD dissertation, 2021, hosted on Figshare per Exa (institution believed Carnegie Mellon University; canonical URL not resolved)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/m0yr2hxbdwf)
- DRL agents in simulated environments, mutants used to grade scenario generators: "we refer to a test environment generator (or test generator TG for short), rather than a test set"; results show "µPRL is effective at discriminating strong from weak test generators, hence providing useful feedback to developers about the adequacy of the generated test scenarios." **[full, partial read]** — [Thomas, Biagiola, Humbatova, Wardat, Jahangirova, Rajan, Tonella, "muPRL: A Mutation Testing Pipeline for Deep Reinforcement Learning based on Real Faults", arXiv preprint dated 2024-08-27 per Exa (venue believed ICSE 2025; arXiv ID not confirmed)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/nw27rwph2ph)

### Inferences
- Half (a) of the claim is not new in this field. "Mutants grade the scenario suite" (Laurent 2019, Birkemeyer 2022, Deng 2022), "mutants grade the scenario generator" (muPRL 2024; Simulink test generators since 2005) and "mutants grade a coverage metric" (Afzal 2021) are all published. Laurent et al. 2019 does it on a deterministic simulator.
- "Mutants grade each invariant/oracle" is less common: PBMT and BERTiMuS score per requirement, DaMAT reads live mutants as oracle weakness, but I found no paper that ranks individual properties by the mutants each one kills.

### Gaps
- I did not find an empirical mutation study that mutates source code of a full ADS stack and reports survivors by cause; Deng et al. report only detection ratios.
- SBFT CPS competition reports were read at abstract level only; whether any edition used seeded faults in the driving agent is unconfirmed.

## 3. Do authors separate "not exercised", "exercised with no output difference", "output differs but property holds" and "property violated"?

### Takeaway
Partly, and in two places. DaMAT (TSE 2023) separates not covered / covered but mutation not applied / applied and live / killed, and reads the live class as oracle weakness after checking that outputs differ. Shin (2026) proposes, without experiments, weakly killed (control trace diverges, same safety outcome) / strongly killed (safety constraint violated) / never weakly killed (approximated as equivalent). PBMT and BERTiMuS give the two-level kill. I found no CPS paper with all four classes, and none that measures reachability of a source-level mutant alongside trace difference and property verdict.

### Cited Findings
- DaMAT's three non-kill cases: "we have defined three metrics ...: fault model coverage, mutation operation coverage, and covered mutation score. These metrics measure the frequency of the following scenarios: (case 1) the message type targeted by a mutant is never exercised, (case 2) the message type is covered by the test suite but it is not possible to perform some of the mutation operations (e.g., because the test suite does not exercise out-of-range cases), (case 3) the mutation is performed but the test suite does not fail." The idea is credited as "Inspired by work on abstract mutation analysis [52]". **[full]** — [Viganò et al., TSE 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TSE.2022.3213041)
- DaMAT ties the last class to the oracle: the covered mutation score "provides information about the quality of test oracles; indeed, a mutant that performs a mutation operation and is not killed (i.e., is live) indicates that the test suite cannot detect the effect of the mutation". The four named shortcomings are "untested message types (UMT), uncovered input partitions (UIP), poor oracle quality (POQ), and lack of test inputs (LTI)". **[full]** — [same](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TSE.2022.3213041)
- DaMAT's reported outcome: "All live mutants (i.e., 55 mutants for ESAIL-ADCS, 1 mutant for ESAIL-GPS, and 45 mutants for LIBP) generate outputs that differ from the original CPS and, therefore, we did not detect any equivalent mutant." and "live mutants (i.e., low MS) suggest poor oracle quality (POQ). In our case study systems the presence of live mutants was not explained by the lack of test inputs". LIBP's covered mutation score was 90.24%. **[full]** — [same](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TSE.2022.3213041)
- Shin's propagation analysis: "Weakly killed: The mutated message successfully propagates through and alters the control module's output state (e.g., the steering angle or throttle trace diverges from the original ADS execution), but results in no difference in safety outcomes compared to the original ADS. Strongly killed: The state divergence forces the mutant to violate a safety constraint that was otherwise maintained in the original execution. If a mutant is never weakly killed by any test scenario in the suite (i.e., the control traces remain identical to the original executions), we can approximate that the mutant is equivalent (safe-by-design)." **[full]** — [Shin, arXiv 2606.26456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)
- Shin on what survivors are for: "surviving mutants provide concrete evidence of test suite blind spots, guiding further scenario generation", with search used "to efficiently find the environmental entities that escalate a weak kill into a strong kill." **[full]** — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)
- Laurent et al. use only the middle measurement (run differs), as a coverage criterion, and note the under-approximation: "If one of the mutants produces a significantly different result ... then the parameter that was mutated is covered by S; if no mutant produces a different result, then it is considered not covered by S. The mutation-based approximation is thus an underestimation of parameter coverage". **[full]** — [Laurent et al., TOSEM 2022 (Exa copy)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/g2911qsmmwz)
- For nondeterministic models, Fellner et al. separate "potential" from "definite" killing: "we discuss mutation analysis in our setting and define potential and definite killing of mutants ... multiple settings in terms of presence or absence of non-determinism". **[full, outline passage only]** — [Fellner, Tabaei Befrouei, Weissenbacher, "Mutation testing with hyperproperties", Software and Systems Modeling, 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10270-020-00850-1)

### Inferences
- The claim's four-way sort maps onto published pieces: "scenario gap" = DaMAT cases 1 and 2 / Laurent's "not covered"; "equivalent on this corpus" = Shin's "never weakly killed"; "oracle gap" = DaMAT's live-with-different-output / Shin's weak kill / BERTiMuS's classically-killed-but-not-requirements-killed; "killed" = PBMT kill. What I did not find is one harness doing all of it for source-level mutants.
- Shin's scheme has no reachability step because message mutants are applied on the wire. DaMAT has a reachability-like step (was the mutation operation applied?) but for data, not code. The claim's "reached the mutated code" measurement for source mutants is the piece without a direct CPS precedent in what I read.
- Shin's "never weakly killed means equivalent" depends on traces being identical on repeat, which Shin says CARLA does not provide. A deterministic simulator removes that obstacle; this is the strongest support in this literature for the claim's point that determinism is what makes the sort cheap.

### Gaps
- Proportions are scarce: DaMAT gives counts of live mutants (55, 1, 45) and one score; Shin gives none; I did not extract BERTiMuS's per-notion kill counts or MASS's breakdown of live mutants.
- I did not check the general mutation literature (RIP/RIPR model, "Leveraging Propagated Infection to Crossfire Mutants" 2024, PISCES testability analysis), which surfaced in q10 and classifies survivors for ordinary programs; it is outside this domain scope and should be checked by whoever covers mainstream mutation testing.
- "Abstract mutation analysis" (DaMAT's reference [52]) was not identified or read.

## 4. Simulator nondeterminism and flakiness when deciding a kill

### Takeaway
Nondeterminism is treated as a fact of life in ADS and robotics simulation and it is large: hard-flaky rates from 1% to 74% across five ADS setups, and 31.3% of benchmark scenarios potentially flaky in CARLA against none in MetaDrive. The standard remedy for mutation is repeated runs plus a statistical kill (Mann-Whitney U with effect size; Fisher's exact test), which multiplies cost; other remedies are flaky-test classifiers, input validators, simulator ensembles, and extracting deterministic module-level tests. Only Laurent et al. 2019 and MetaDrive show the deterministic case in passing; nobody in this set argues from determinism as an asset for mutation.

### Cited Findings
- Size, ADS: "for our five test setups, 4%-68% of the generated tests exhibit notable variations in fitness values ... Further, the hard flaky rate for these setups ranges from 1% to 74%, with four out of five exhibiting a hard flaky rate exceeding 6% for at least one fitness function." Hard flaky = "passes and fails non-deterministically over multiple re-executions"; soft flaky = "variations in the values of a fitness function". Both simulators were run "in their synchronous mode" to reduce randomness. An ML classifier flags flaky tests from one run with F1 of 85%, 82% and 96%. **[full]** — [Amini, Naseri, Nejati, "Evaluating the Impact of Flaky Simulators on Testing Autonomous Driving Systems", Empirical Software Engineering 2024 (ICSE 2025 journal-first), arXiv 2311.18768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18768)
- Size, CARLA vs MetaDrive: "31.3% of benchmark test scenarios are potentially flaky due to nondeterministic simulations in CARLA, whereas MetaDrive does not yield any flaky tests." **[abstract]** — [Osikowicz, McMinn, Shin, "Empirically Evaluating Flaky Tests for Autonomous Driving Systems in Simulated Environments", IEEE/ACM Flaky Tests Workshop 2025, pp. 13-20 (Exa copy)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/78pssb0q44w)
- Cause and bound: game-engine simulation has an operating domain "where the simulation precision is sufficiently high i.e. variance between repeated executions becomes negligible". **[abstract]** Exa's summary gives actor-path deviations of up to about 59 cm unrestricted and about 1 cm under restricted resource use. **[summary; figures not verified]** — [Chance, Ghobrial, McAreavey, Lemaignan, Pipe, Eder, "On Determinism of Game Engines Used for Simulation-Based Autonomous Vehicle Verification", IEEE T-ITS 23(11), 2022, DOI 10.1109/TITS.2022.3177887](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9793395)
- Effect on mutation, stated directly: "This nondeterminism can cause natural state divergences, making it difficult to determine whether a mutant is killed. Future research must investigate statistical methods, such as threshold-based equivalence checking, to reliably assess propagation analysis over multiple simulator iterations with statistically rigorous confidence intervals." **[full]** — [Shin, arXiv 2606.26456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26456)
- Statistical kill in ADS mutation: "If a difference is detected, is it due to the change in value of the parameter or simply to the uncertainty in the simulation? This work uses multiple runs from both versions of the ADS and compares them using statistical methods". Rule: "covered_metr(S, p) = p-value ≤ 0.05 ∧ (Â12 ≤ 0.362 ∨ Â12 ≥ 0.638)" using "A Mann-Whitney U test" and Vargha-Delaney effect size; "the cost of computing coverage can be managed with suitable heuristics." **[full]** — [Laurent et al., TOSEM 2022 (Exa copy)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/g2911qsmmwz)
- Statistical kill for learned agents: "we adopt the definition of statistical killing [31]: a mutant is killed by a test set if the prediction accuracies of original and mutated model computed on such test set differ in a statistically significant way"; muPRL trains n instances per mutant and uses Fisher's exact test, and defines "killable (and its complement, likely-equivalent)" mutation configurations. **[full, partial read]** — [Thomas et al., muPRL (Exa copy)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/nw27rwph2ph)
- Robotics: "Due to the non-determinism of the robot control and simulation execution, a test might fail or pass with exactly the same inputs (same world and mission) ... test input diversity is more efficient than repeated runs in order to activate faults." **[abstract]** — [Robert, Guiochet, Waeselynck, "Testing a non-deterministic robot in simulation - How many repeated runs?", 2020 (Exa copy; venue not confirmed, believed IEEE IRC 2020)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/7rgnvbycp59)
- arXiv 2508.20902 is titled "Automated Test Validators for Flaky Cyber-Physical System Simulators: Approach and Evaluation" (Jodat, Gaaloul, Sabetzadeh, Nejati), not "test oracles". It filters inputs before execution and does not decide kills: "we propose to use test validators to filter out such test inputs before execution"; validators are "robust against flakiness with only 4% average variation in their accuracy results across four different network and autonomous-driving systems with flaky behaviours." **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2508.20902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20902)
- DaMAT removes nondeterminism from its own operators: "to eliminate non-determinism, engineers can configure BF to mutate all the bits in the specified set". **[full]** — [Viganò et al., TSE 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TSE.2022.3213041)
- Other mitigations: simulator ensembles (Sorokin, Biagiola, Stocco, 2025/2026), "digital siblings" (EMSE 2024), and DeFT, which extracts deterministic planning-module tests from nondeterministic system-level runs (Huai et al., 2026). **[summary]** — q05, q19, q22 raw results; [digital siblings, EMSE 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-024-10458-4)

### Inferences
- The field's cost of nondeterminism for mutation is concrete: n runs of the original and n of every mutant per scenario, plus a significance threshold that turns small real effects into "not covered". A seeded deterministic simulator needs one run each and can use exact trace equality, which is what Shin's equivalence approximation and Laurent's 2019 version assume.
- MetaDrive's zero flaky tests and Chance et al.'s "permissible variance" domain show that determinism is a property of the simulator design, not of the domain.

### Gaps
- No paper found that measures how often flakiness flips a mutant's kill verdict. Shin names it as open work.
- I did not read the Osikowicz et al. full text (causes and mitigation list) or verify the Chance et al. figures.

## 5. Mutation analysis of simulation models themselves; mutants to compare scenario generators or select scenarios

### Takeaway
Mutation of simulation models exists but is thin: operators for agent-based models (2010, proposal only), mutation of queueing-network performance models run by discrete-event simulation (JSS 2022, JSIMutate), an early use of mutants to verify error-isolation matrices for a simulation model (2003), and model-based mutation of timed automata and action systems. Mutants used to compare generators and to select scenarios are covered in section 2 (Birkemeyer 2022, Deng 2022, muPRL 2024, Arrieta 2018-2025, Xiao and Menzies 2021, Laurent 2019).

### Cited Findings
- Agent-based models: "agent-based models are an independent class of software application with their own unique properties, with the consequential need for the definition of suitable, tailored mutation operators ... mutation operators are proposed that can imitate potential programmer errors and result in faulty simulation runs of a model." Short position paper. **[abstract]** — [Adra, McMinn, "Mutation Operators for Agent-Based Models", 2010 (Exa copy; ICST workshop venue not confirmed)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/flttrqc3khb)
- Queueing networks simulated as discrete-event models: mutation operators change queue size, number of servers and queueing strategy; the paper states "we apply mutation analysis to models" and defines mutants as detected when they "produce results different from the expected ones". The purpose is to judge whether a workload is informative about performance, not to test code. **[full, related-work passage only; the rest from summary]** — [Laurent, Arcaini, Trubiani, Ventresque, Journal of Systems and Software 191 (2022) 111385](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2022.111385); tool: [JSIMutate (ESEC/FSE 2022 demo), repository](https://github.com/ucd-csl/QueueingNetworksMutation)
- Mutants used to check a debugging aid for a wastewater-treatment simulation model. **[summary]** — [Carter, Graham, Strooper, Yuan, "Mutation analysis to verify feature matrices for isolating errors in simulation models", 2003 (Exa copy)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/c5h0c0t2q3k)
- Timed-automata and real-time models: Aichernig, Lorber, Ničković, "Model-based Mutation Testing with Timed Automata" (2013); Lorber, Larsen, Nielsen, "Model-Based Mutation Testing of Real-Time Systems via Model Checking" (2018). Kill by timed conformance. **[summary]** — q14, q16 raw results
- Validation of simulation models more often uses metamorphic testing than mutation (Olsen and Raunak 2018; Farhan et al. 2021), sometimes with mutants as the yardstick for the metamorphic relations. **[summary]** — q06 raw results

### Inferences
- For discrete-event simulation the published mutation work targets the model of the system (queueing network, agent rules), with performance or output distributions as the observable. I found nothing that treats a deterministic discrete-event simulator as the harness for source-level mutation of the software under test with trace invariants as the oracle; that combination sits in the distributed-systems simulation literature, which another researcher covers.

### Gaps
- No DEVS-specific mutation testing paper was found (q21 returned a DEVS black-box test frame without mutants).
- Winter Simulation Conference and SIMULATION-journal literature was not searched by venue.
