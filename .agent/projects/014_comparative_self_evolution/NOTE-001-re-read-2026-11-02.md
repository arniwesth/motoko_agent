## 0. Why re-read now

RESEARCH is 81 days old. In that interval `main` gained 1,053 commits, the pinned AILANG moved
from v0.33.0 to v0.54.14, and the projects directory grew from 014 to 041. Four of the new
projects built or decided things RESEARCH listed as gaps: an admission gate with an evaluator the
candidate does not own (013 ADR-004), a governed contract tier (027), a judged corpus and a
changed oracle (011 ADR-003), and a standing mutation discipline (meta-decision of 2026-10-04,
applied in 034 and 041). On the day this note was written a sibling session re-read 011 the same
way and ran 039's pilot (PILOT); §3.3 carries its result. One later document — DIRECTION —
states the project's theme in words that overlap RESEARCH §3.4 almost sentence for sentence,
without either citing the other. None of that was in view when RESEARCH was written and RESEARCH
has not been touched since.

This note asks what still holds, what does not, and what the adoption order should be today. It
changes nothing in RESEARCH: per 027's and 013's convention, the original stays as the record of
what was believed and corrections live here.

Two things it is not. It is not a plan — the three candidate decisions are in §8. And it is not
a claim that any evolution loop exists: nothing automated mutates Motoko today, and §2's last two
rows say what the nearest things are.

## 3. What changed the picture

### 3.1 The evaluator the candidate does not own exists, and it is narrower than §3.4 asked for

RESEARCH §3.4 proposed three constitutional rules. ADR-004 D5 instantiates each, for its own
scope, in a shape RESEARCH did not predict:

| RESEARCH §3.4 rule | ADR-004 D5 | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Within an epoch, `dst_*.ail`, profiles, catalogue and invariants are frozen | a **protected closure** — the dependency closure of `recording_ports`' bindings and the two seams' delegates — pinned by exact-span hash of the original bytes at A, reviewed with the manifest; what is *not* frozen is listed on purpose (`session.ail`'s loop, `phase_vocab.ail`, `journal.ail`, `step_machine.ail`, `tool_phase.ail`) | narrower (a closure, not a directory), mechanised (a checker, not a rule), and it protects the **recorder and evaluator**. `src/core/dst_*.ail` is on D3's refusal list for candidates (`candidate.py` `D3_PATHS`), so a candidate cannot touch the oracle — but nothing versions the oracle on an admission. |
| 2. Oracle changes only at boundaries, gated by the previous oracle plus replay-identity | "changing any of [the load-bearing pieces] costs a **re-admission**" (Consequences); the same-basis control (D8 P3) must be rejected by the new gate while K1–K7 pass | the same rule, stated as *basis change ⇒ re-admit* rather than as epochs; the "previous oracle" check is ADR-003 ruling 14's row-for-row matrix comparison, done once, by hand |
| 3. The lineage records which oracle epoch each admission ran under | seven identities per entry — manifest, snapshot, excerpt, selector, program, assembly, execution — plus the `ailang` binary hash | satisfied, for ADR-004 entries |

The explicit non-claim matters as much as the mechanism: *"candidates are cooperative and
reviewed … A candidate that edits the recorder to lie consistently is excluded by review, not
detected. No adversarial guarantee is claimed; stronger isolation is Not decided"*
(`ADR-004:1125–1130`). RESEARCH §3.4 wanted Goodhart *resistance*. What exists is Goodhart
*detection for ordinary omissions* plus a trust model that names review as the adversarial
defence. For a loop whose editor is the subject (RESEARCH §7 Q4), that is the open end.

DIRECTION says the principle in prose: *"a proposed successor must earn acceptance against
evidence and criteria it cannot rewrite during its own evaluation … This does not require
freezing the entire evaluation system forever. It requires explicit transitions between
standards, with an account of what changed and why the supporting evidence is sufficient."* That
is RESEARCH §3.4 rules 1 and 2 without the epoch mechanics, and ADR-003 is its first instance.
Nothing has turned it into a recorded field on an admission. §8 C1 proposes that.

### 3.2 "Minutes-scale" was wrong, and the gate is three tiers, not two

RESEARCH §3.1 called the DST suite "deterministic, seeded, minutes-scale" and §5 claimed
"near-free admission". Measured since (all *inherited* from 011's spike, 2026-10-06, at v0.47.2):

| Tier | Cost | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| contracts (`verify_core`) | ~11 ms per contract, linear; worst single contract 73 ms; the module parse dominates at ~350 ms each (027 §5) | the proof-adjacent gate |
| `corpus_judge` | **40–51 s per mutant** | the thirteen families and five two-channel checks on every corpus member's real run |
| targeted DST set | 3.7 min per mutant | the targets the spike found relevant |
| full `make dst` | **~30 min warm (`-j4`), ~50 min cold (`-j8`)**; 51 h per 100 mutants | all 55 targets; `session.ail` exceeds AILANG's 16 MiB compile-cache limit, so every mutant recompiles it |
| `eval_matrix` | 25–40 min, memory-guarded, sequential | the evaluator's own test contract |
| ADR-004 long admission | exclusive execution, 7 GiB RSS class (prototype) | a 300-call entry |

Two consequences. The cost inversion RESEARCH §3.1 claimed (O(mutants × DST) in place of
O(mutants × benchmark)) survives only as a *tiered* claim: contracts and `corpus_judge` are the
cheap pre-filter; the full sweep is a benchmark-scale cost of its own. And 035's reviewer was
right to refuse "the historical 196-second timing as the current budget" — every cost figure in
RESEARCH §6 must be re-measured before a plan is cut on it.

SIFT (§1) is the lineage's answer to the same cost: replace most evaluations with an LLM judge.
Motoko's answer is to replace them with an oracle set that is deterministic but, as §3.3 shows,
of partly measured and bounded strength. The bets differ in what they trust; neither is free.

### 3.3 The oracle has a strength, and it was measured

RESEARCH treated the oracle risk as *tampering* (§3.4). 011 found the other risk first: two
one-edit mutants in recovery branches — a retried stream error consuming no step budget, a
non-retryable provider failure finalising as success — **survived the whole of `make dst`**, and
"the invariant families caught none of them on a real run" (spike note, *inherited*). ADR-003's
answer was two rule changes and a gate, each rule accepted only when its named mutant was seen
red, with blind mutants added by a reviewer (D6).

039 names the discipline *oracle qualification* — source mutants, inside the deterministic
world, grading the invariant families, the generator and the corpus, each mutant sorted
non-activated / non-propagated / non-detected / detected (§8.1 proposed a pilot of about thirty
seeded mutants on `corpus_judge`). The house rule is one level down: every part mutates each rule
it claims once and names the test that must fail (meta-decision; 034's three runs; 041's four
workstreams).

**The pilot ran on 2026-11-02** (PILOT, *inherited*; on a branch, not yet on `main`). Thirty
sites drawn by seed from 87 eligible production sites in `session`, `step_machine`, `recovery`
and `tool_phase`, edits and predictions hash-pinned before any run, a 120 s observation deadline:

| First-attempt result | Count |
|---|---|
| a named `corpus_judge` rule red on a bank member | 5 |
| red only on a separate control, rejected by a reach guard, or incomplete at the deadline | 4 |
| bank wire differs, no judge finding — later caught by `ledger_parity` (3), `corpus_pr` (1), `journal_resume` (1) | 5 |
| no observed wire difference (equivalents, restricted states, and one off-world effect) | 16 |

Four things in it bear on RESEARCH, each stated by PILOT as an observation and not a score:

- **The gate is a set of oracles with different observation channels, not a judge.** Kills
  were spread over `corpus_judge`, `ledger_parity`, `corpus_pr` and `journal_resume`; a dropped
  emission leaves the returned trace intact and is visible only to the stdout-parity gate.
  PILOT's disposition is to keep them distinct — "not a reason to funnel every obligation into
  one judge". RESEARCH §3.1's "the DST profile suite" was one box; it is several, and a loop's
  admission rule has to name which.
- **One mutant's effect is off-world.** M12 flips a debug-capture flag; the mutant and the clean
  source emit identical wire output, and M12 writes `.motoko/failing_payload.json` through
  native FS. No judge reads that filesystem. So "still replays" does not mean "same behaviour":
  the world boundary leaks where a helper writes ambiently, and the fix PILOT names is a
  capture-off/on filesystem fixture, not another invariant.
- **Four of the five named kills were not predicted** by the authors who chose the mutants. An
  oracle whose own authors mis-forecast which rule fires is not one an LLM-written defect
  hypothesis (Φ_RM) can be checked against by reasoning; it has to be run.
- **Cost**: 2,265 s aggregate for four controls and thirty attempts, about 46 s of judge time
  per mutant — the `corpus_judge` tier of §3.2, confirmed on the current toolchain.

For RESEARCH this changes the meaning of "still passes DST" in §3.1. A gate admits only what it
can see; the first bounded measurement of what it sees is in, and the whole-suite yield is not
(PILOT: "The full DST sweep and a whole-suite mutation yield remain unmeasured"). "Admitted"
still means "not caught by an oracle set of partly measured kill rate". §5 carries this as G8.

Two further observations from the mutation records bear on an automated loop:

- *Passing is not evidence. Discrimination is the evidence* (013 GATE). A loop that writes its
  own tests must also show each test fail on its mutant. The author chooses the mutants today;
  if the author is the loop, that choice is a Goodhart surface. ADR-003 D6's blind reviewer
  mutants are the existing counter, and they are a human.
- 039's thesis note names a check nobody has built — mutate the *check* and ask whether the
  tests would pass with a weaker one (vacuity) — which is RESEARCH §3.4's "mutate a validator to
  admit its own descendant" stated as a test rather than a prohibition.

### 3.4 The contract tier went from two sites to a governed register

RESEARCH §3.5 and §7 Q5 asked whether contract-widening repays its annotation cost and which
modules go first. Both are answered, by policy rather than by study: every new `pure func` in
`src/core/` carries a contract or a checked excuse (027 ADR-001 §4), and `verify_survey` answers
"where next" by synthesising a trivial contract on each function and reading the verdict. The
register's classification is solver-computed — *proven* is not the metric, *substantive* is — so
RESEARCH's "verified-property admission" has a measure that cannot be gamed by tautologies: 73
substantive of 76.

Its ceiling is also known now, and RESEARCH's candidates did not respect it:

- Folds, closures and effectful bodies are outside the fragment (029: "permanent for an
  SMT-only path"). `compaction.ail` and `c2_loop`, which RESEARCH §7 Q5 named, are made of them.
  `compress_output` is blocked on an unencodable builtin (`std/string.split`).
- `ailang verify` encodes `float` as an SMT real: no NaN, so a verified float equality can be
  false at runtime (AILANG LIMITATIONS, added 2026-09-25).
- 029 observed `make verify_core` red once in nine runs on 2026-09-02, with a Z3 `unknown`
  printed as `ERROR` (filed as MOT-138). This session's single run was green; the flakiness was
  not re-measured (§9).
- The tier is a **pull-request gate, not a loop gate**: DP7 is gone (005 Amendment 1, "there is
  no scenario in which Motoko's own `make check_core` should be triggered automatically on a
  final answer"), and `verify_core` is not folded into `check_core` until 027 §5's three
  conditions hold (touched-file scoping, an honest timeout message, a pinned verified-set).

So §3.5's "middle tier no paper occupies" exists as a mechanism, at ~11 ms per contract, and
cannot yet be a step inside an automated admission.

### 3.5 Φ_CH's causal localisation exists, as the evaluator's verdict

RESEARCH §3.3's novel claim was *first-divergent-world-request* localisation. That is now
literally what ADR-004 returns: `Diverged { location, finding }` with locations
`Interaction(ordinal)`, `Call(k)` and `Aggregate(...)`, over a program whose every served
response has a recorded interaction behind it (ADR-004 Context, item 1). The prerequisites moved:

- 013 §2.A's "unified vocabulary" landed as the **ordinal** (six request classes, one writer,
  witnesses drained at frame boundaries) rather than as a defunctionalised `WorldRequest` sum;
  013 NOTE-002 demoted defunctionalisation to seventh and recorded that §2.A "gained two
  consumers", one of them RESEARCH §3.3.
- The census is 10 of 13, not total, and the three unrecorded classes are reads with a stated
  reason. A divergence in `path_stat` or `dir_list` is still invisible to the diff.
- Shrinking (011 ADR-001) is still Proposed and unimplemented (035, *inherited*).

Two caveats RESEARCH stated now have authority behind them. DIRECTION: *"Replaying an old
response against a materially different request cannot be treated as evidence of the new
behavior's quality."* And 023: replay "certifies determinism of the recorded universe" — the
recorder grading itself one level up. So Φ_CH-via-replay reports *where two variants diverge*,
not *which is better*; RESEARCH §3.3's triage split (replay-diffable vs model-dependent) stands,
and §7 Q3 is still unmeasured. It is cheaper to measure than it was: ADR-004's admission turns a
real session into a program without touching the live path.

### 3.6 The unit of variation is contested

RESEARCH §7 Q2 asked whether a variant is a commit, a branch or a (commit × profile) pair. Three
answers now exist, and they disagree:

| Source | Unit | Keyed by |
|---|---|---|
| ADR-004 | an **assembly**: candidate tree + evaluator E + toolchain, with execution provenance | seven identities and the binary hash |
| AILANG's package loop | a **profile**: which `motoko_ext_*` packages load | `ext_set_digest`, `system_prefix_digest` and the boot inputs, already in the journal header (030) |
| HSI (2608.08466) | a **harness** swapped through a fixed injection point | — |

HGM's clade statistics need a parent link, and only the first unit has one (parent P and
candidate C in `candidate.py run`). The second is the cheapest to A/B and the one with data. A
lineage index (G2) has to say which it keys on, and the answer is probably both: an assembly is
a node, a profile is a configuration of a node, and CMP folds over nodes.

### 3.7 The direction document absorbs §0

DIRECTION states three levels — Execution, Discovery, Verification — with evidence of progress
for each, defines "verified" as three distinct claims (reproducibility, scoped correctness,
improvement), and names a first demonstration: Motoko finds a blind spot in its own tests, builds
the simulation that exposes it, repairs the defect, and the successor finds further unseen
defects better than its parent, with a version that has the repair but not the test improvement
as the control. RESEARCH §0's two questions are the Execution and Verification rows of that
table. The lineage maps onto the levels: DGM, HGM and MGM evolve at Execution with selection at
Discovery; Hyperagents makes Discovery editable; RQGM co-evolves Verification.

032 (Dream-RSI, 2026-09-19) is the only later note to propose a selection loop. Its Codex review
is the document to carry forward: "ADR-004 is an evaluation design, not a working selection
loop"; the real-journal runner "prints `ADMISSION refused ran=false`" at that HEAD; the
incumbent-as-candidate floor "is not cheap/free" and "cannot certify verifier quality"; a scalar
score may rank candidates that pass the invariants but may not be the acceptance signal; and a
scheduler needs "a durable per-attempt ledger, per-attempt branch witnesses and per-attempt
cost" — RESEARCH's G1 and G2 in other words. 015's three notes extend RESEARCH without
contradiction: the idea factory is "the general case of the mutation operator", and "014's G1
keyed matrix provides the lineage data that HGM-style clade selection requires".

### 3.8 AILANG moved 21 minor versions; four things bear on this project

- **`ailang run --policy restricted`** (M-EXECUTOR-POLICY-HARDENING, v0.41.0): a confined
  executor for the mediated effects, with its residuals written down (no CPU/memory bound
  without a cgroup; `trusted_host` is a grant, not a claim). This is the first concrete answer
  to §7 Q4's "does the editor run inside Motoko": an in-Motoko editor can now be confined by
  the language runtime, which did not exist in August.
- **Effect replay contracts** landed partial (Rand only; Clock/FS deferred). 012's EVAL
  (2026-09-05): not adopted, by design — the registry works at (effect, mode) granularity and
  Motoko's strict replay at world/program granularity. RESEARCH §3.5's "012's registry gives the
  mechanism" holds only as a record of what is tracked.
- **Hidden tests and a per-benchmark exec timeout** in AILANG's own eval (v0.55.0): solutions
  that passed the one visible test survived the hidden ones 3 of 14 and 2 of 17 times, against
  10 of 15 and 9 of 12 for Python. A one-sample oracle overstated one arm far more than the
  other. That is §7 Q1 in miniature: whatever fitness Motoko adopts needs held-out cases, and
  the Polyglot runner's `pass_1 / pass_2` is one sample per task.
- **The verifier's float model** (§3.4 above). `m-motoko-verify-ail.md` still records an
  agent-invocable `ailang verify` runtime mode as its blocker; whether v0.54 ships one was not
  re-checked (§9).

Also on the AILANG side: `m-motoko-self-improvement-loop.md` is a second loop design for the
same harness, package-shaped, with the finding that single-trial A/B is noise-dominated. It and
RESEARCH do not cite each other. Its DP7 arm is gone from core, so it is partly stale too.

### 3.9 Benchmarks did not move, and the fitness signal got a floor

No Polyglot run since 2026-05-31; `python` only; no variant key. 025 (2026-08-24) says a
three-point delta on the Python track "is noise without seeds" and that step count is the one
low-variance metric; AILANG's own matrix found 7 of 26 benchmarks flipping between profiles at a
single trial. RESEARCH §6's "any internal fitness trend is fine" is too generous: an internal
trend needs seeds and N ≥ 3 per cell, which no benchmark run here has had.

## 4. Premises that did not survive

1. **"Motoko has the strongest admission substrate in the field."** Strength is now a measured
   quantity, and the first measurement found two one-edit recovery mutants the whole sweep could
   not see (§3.3), and the second found the kills spread over four gates with one off-world
   effect none of them sees. The substrate is large; its whole-suite yield is unknown.
2. **"Deterministic, minutes-scale."** Minutes is `corpus_judge`. The sweep is half an hour to
   fifty minutes, and no workflow runs it (§3.2).
3. **"005 is the prerequisite for the mutation-surface allowlist."** 005's scope was the
   policy/mechanism seam; both amendments are accepted and all three workstreams landed, and
   none of it is a write-set. G5 has no owning project. The only write-set mechanism in the tree
   is ADR-004 D5's protected closure, and it protects the evaluator, not the oracle.
4. **"Contracts at two sites, a proof of concept."** Seventy-six, governed, in CI (§3.4).
5. **"MGM's numbers are unverified."** Verified, with the -60 subset correction (§1).
6. **"The Goodhart hazard is live."** Confirmed rather than refuted: the oracle was changed
   (ADR-003 D2, D3), and the change was governed by rulings, named mutants and a row-for-row
   matrix comparison. The hazard did not materialise; the governance that prevented it is a
   convention, not a recorded field.
7. **"Replay-identity makes admission causal."** It makes *divergence* causal (§3.5). Quality is
   a separate claim DIRECTION and 023 both refuse to let replay carry.
8. **"The archive is the cheapest, highest-leverage gap."** Still cheap; no longer first. A keyed
   matrix over an oracle set whose strength is only partly measured, and whose membership no
   document names, ranks variants by a signal nobody has qualified.

Not retracted: the layering of the lineage; the adoption order *archive before automation*; the
rejection of in-memory self-patching; the Φ_RM/012 rhyme; the compute-reality caveat, now with
numbers.

## 7. Open questions, restated

1. **Fitness.** Sharper: a deterministic *resource* fitness exists (ADR-004 D4) and a
   deterministic *task* fitness does not. A composite still re-introduces the oracle into the
   utility (RESEARCH §7 Q1), and now also re-introduces an oracle set of partly measured
   strength (G8).
2. **Variant granularity.** Three units in play (§3.6). The lineage index should name the
   assembly as the node and the profile as its configuration; the journal header already keys
   the second.
3. **What fraction of benchmark failures reproduce under scripted worlds?** Still unmeasured.
   Cheaper now (ADR-004 admission); still blocked on there being a benchmark run at all.
4. **Who runs the editor?** The practice is Claude and Codex delegates under herdr, in their own
   worktrees, each part shipping mutation evidence. The language now offers confinement for an
   in-Motoko editor. The open end is §3.1's: review is the adversarial defence, and a loop whose
   editor is the subject removes the reviewer.
5. **Contract-widening ROI.** Answered by policy (every new pure function) and by tool
   (`verify_survey`); the remaining question is folding the verified-set into a gate that runs
   inside something, which 027 §5 conditions on three things.
6. **New: what is the oracle set's whole-suite yield, by gate and by family?** PILOT gives the
   first bounded count (5 + 4 of 30, no score) and declines to grow the population; the
   whole-suite number every rank in §6 depends on is still open.
7. **New: how does an admission record which oracle judged it?** A field, a digest of
   `dst_invariants.ail` and the catalogue version, or the `eval_matrix` expected-file hash —
   §8 C1's content.
8. **New: can the mutation discipline survive the author being the loop?** ADR-003 D6's blind
   reviewer mutants are the only existing counter and they are human.
