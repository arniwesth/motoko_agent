## 2. TL;DR 1 — outcome reuse is shared; the tested layer and coverage need precision

**What stands.** D2 says replay does not call the generator (D:699); the paper says replay reveals saved outcomes without rerunning either discovery agent or evaluator (P:176–197). Motoko's execution bridge takes a real `TracedSessionResult` into `ExecutionUnderTest`, and `evaluate` calls thirteen families (`src/core/dst_execution.ail:100–130`; `src/core/dst_invariants.ail:2010–2024`). Dream-RSI updates exploration-policy code while keeping models, evaluator and execution interfaces fixed (P:190–194). These are different objects of evaluation, and the note correctly identifies that distinction.

**What must change.** N:62–63 calls the exploration orchestration layer a harness “untested by anything in the paper.” Its policy behavior is the object of the replay evaluations and live comparisons (P:228–318, :327–344). The justified criticism is that the paper reports no Motoko-style conformance/fault-injection study of the orchestration and recording machinery. Performance testing of a controller and correctness testing of its executor are different evidence; do not erase the former to emphasize the latter.

The opening's all-profile claim (N:14–17), followed by “compaction, hooks” (N:60–62), also needs the actual scope: `driver_only` installs no extensions, `driver_plus_no_ops` has no substantive world mediation, and the report's compose witness is one hook in one session (S:42–53; T:589–640, :753–766). ADR-004 T0 separately excludes compaction and extensions (A:1079–1083, :1361). Architecture can accommodate a seam without every named profile exercising it.

**Corrected claim.** Both replay systems reuse realized outcomes. Dream-RSI evaluates an exploration controller over a fixed coding/evaluation setup; Motoko exercises production harness behavior within each profile's measured boundary. The paper does not report a corresponding harness-conformance assessment (D:2337–2343; P:176–197; T:577–640).

## 3. TL;DR 2 — the analogy is useful, but refusal, visibility and readiness are different axes

ADR-004 is structurally related: a frozen workload, candidate execution, a fixed evaluator, and explicit comparisons. But its program is **recorded from a parent admission run over a constrained journal-built world**, not a complete recording of the live environment. The journal and required host excerpt supply only part of that world's data; settings and synthetic outcomes supply the rest (A:532–606, :754–780, :883–917). Its input is the corpus entry, including the snapshot-held seed, not the program alone (A:908–913).

Three load-bearing corrections follow.

1. **The strongest distinction is evaluation contract, not a demonstrated silent failure in Dream-RSI.** ADR-004 requires complete agreement on named observations before crediting a whole-process resource measurement. Dream-RSI deliberately values subsets of a recorded search space. The paper does not specify that an unsupported grid plan receives numeric zero, produces no diagnostic, or is confused with a voluntary stop (A:1036–1103; P:253–263, :1163–1177, :1212–1222). §8.3 tests the inference explicitly.
2. **A hash-pinned evaluator is not a prefix-only observation interface.** D5 protects the meaning and provenance of evaluation code; it neither defines revealed/unrevealed search nodes nor hides future observations from a candidate. It expressly provides witnesses under cooperative review, not adversarial isolation (A:1122–1130, :1132–1219). This invalidates the seventh row of N §3.1 and its repeated “two-phase version of D5” argument at N:361–363.
3. **Design availability is not completed deployment.** N:267 acknowledges waiting for D8, but N:64–71 still reads as an operational claim. At HEAD, `RealEntry` admission dispatches unconditionally to a function that prints `ADMISSION refused ran=false` and exits 1; the candidate path also refuses `EVAL_ENTRY` (`scripts/eval/journal_replay.ail:209–215,234–243,291–300`). The reusable replay machinery and substantial synthetic evaluator code exist; a functioning real-journal selection loop does not follow from them.

The resource characterization is correct only with D4's scope intact: cumulative allocation of the **whole named evaluator process**, including its seed fold and checks; GC, heap, RSS and recursion are secondary; no task-quality, latency or provider claim (A:1105–1118). That is a substantive difference from maximizing partial search utility, not merely a different coefficient in one common objective.

## 4. TL;DR 3 — keep the generator distinction, restore regression's compatibility mechanism

**Generative contrast accepted.** Dream-RSI offline replay reveals saved children; obtaining new outcomes requires online discovery (P:217–263). Motoko discovery makes request-dependent seeded choices; this permits a new execution program for changed code instead of inventing responses to unrecorded requests (D:693–699, :748–753). The direction doc expressly requires responsive simulation and live transfer experiments alongside replay (R:53).

**A seed alone is not the reproduction key.** The contract pins schema, generator id/version, seed, initial world, manifest and profile; the manifest includes source revision and configuration (D:2262–2264, :2274–2309; `src/core/dst_profile.ail:1474–1490`). N §2 row 4's bare `(generator id, version, seed)` and TL;DR's “function of a seed” should be qualified. Re-discovery under changed requests is a new experiment, not recovery of the old live/model behavior.

**The missed mechanism is the strict/regression split itself.** Regression may continue on compatible identity while recording request-projection differences (D:765–772). At HEAD, both `ProjectionDiffers` and `OutcomeDiffers` are nonfatal; kind, origin, unsafe identity, exhaustion and unused interactions remain fatal (`src/core/dst_replay.ail:209–218,319–386,438–442`). The isolated execution in §8.4 prints `projection_fatal=false` and `identity_fatal=true`. Thus “a changed harness diverges, so fails closed” skips the designed compatibility case. The implementation grades two completed logs; do not relabel that comparison an online sandbox that prevents the candidate from forming a request.

**Dream-RSI's claims also need narrowing.** Stochastic transitions mean the paper supplies no deterministic online regeneration contract; they do not prove a tree “cannot” ever be regenerated (P:223–225). The runtime can receive an unsupported grid request—the prompt explicitly describes one—so N:275 and :293–294's impossibility language is too strong (P:1163–1169). Its exactness is replay of stored empirical outcomes, not proof that another live schedule would generate the same outcomes: B.1 even gives discovery attempts access to sibling and historical proposals (P:912–915).

## 5. TL;DR 4 — transfer the experimental structure, not its claimed guarantees

**Candidate §4.1: conditionally useful, with more evidence plumbing than stated.** `CorpusMember` already has both keys, manifest, seed axes, classes and branches. But an in-memory member with class-derived branch labels is not an independently witnessed durable run-yield record (`src/core/dst_corpus.ail:238–293`; `scripts/dst/corpus_rotating_dst.ail:270–301`). §8.6 inventories the available fields and missing observations. The proposed action “which fault families next” is also a new scheduler/generator interface: today's rotating runner's input is a seed, and its profile/generator/bounds are chosen by the runner, not by a family-selection action (`scripts/dst/corpus_rotating_dst.ail:160–175,290–301,385`). Restrict an initial experiment to choosing recorded seed/configuration cases, or explicitly design the added action.

Branch reach per seed is a defensible **coverage-efficiency proxy**. It is not “defects found per compute budget”: a correct implementation can execute a recovery branch, and a checker can miss an incorrect recovery (R:59; T:769–777). Preserve branch identities and marginal union coverage as diagnostics; a scalar ranking may still use a set's cardinality. N:501–503's prohibition on using its size does not follow from equal-cardinality counterexamples. The problem is losing identities or treating reach as detection, not the existence of a scalar objective.

**Incumbent inclusion: accept only under a fixed comparison.** Equation (1)'s guarantee follows immediately because the maximum is taken over a set containing policy 0 on the same history and objective (P:298–318). It guarantees neither per-tree non-regression nor out-of-sample improvement. For verifier transitions, first require the independent correctness/admissibility checks, then compare parent and candidate with the same evaluation version, evidence and resource accounting; retain known-correct cases as well as faulty ones (R:57–65, :76–78). A changed metric, selectively dropped refusals, or candidate-selected evidence invalidates the claimed common floor. Computing the parent and preserving that basis are work, even though the final inequality is cheap.

**Candidate §4.3: preserve the interface, replace the enforcement claim.** A pure `solve(prefix, legal_actions, fixed_config)` and a separate `plan(completed_history, caps)` are sensible proposals (P:983–989, :1088–1098, :1156–1177). A type that permits FS does not distinguish a permitted manifest from an unrevealed trace; a pure function can inspect any future data handed to it as a value. Withholding those values and capabilities is the critical design obligation. D5 then protects the evaluator/adapter closure under its stated trust model; it does not implement information-flow control (A:1122–1130; T:745–751). Seeded, explicitly threaded randomization can also remain reproducible; Appendix B's “never sample randomly” is a design choice, not a necessary condition for reproducibility (`src/core/dst_generator.ail:44–60`).

N:86 and :473 point to §4.3 for selection, but §4.3 contains the prefix interface, not an incumbent-selection design. Give that proposal its own explicit placement and contract.

## 6. TL;DR 5 — two valid limitations, two overstatements

**No verifier evolution:** accepted as a description of the central method. The paper holds models, evaluator and execution interfaces fixed, and R proposes improving verification too (P:190–194; R:33–39). Do not turn that into “measurement cannot weaken” (N:342–346): correctness of the fixed measurement and generalization still need evidence (R:51, :61). Appendix B explicitly warns that a legacy AUC-only objective is not numerically comparable with the current reward (P:1205–1210).

**No support accounting:** reject as a blanket statement. The paper defines revealed subtrees, explicit termination causes, legal roots/frontiers, per-observation status, trace dimensions, planned/effective grids, opened width/depth, work and rounds, and per-decision archived replay traces (P:243–263, :983–995, :1163–1177, :1212–1222). I found no published normalized unsupported-request rate or Motoko-style independent completeness gate. Those are narrower absences. An intentional early stop is not evidence of a defective recorder or failed support: stopping is part of the objective (P:264–297).

**No transfer test:** reject without the qualifier “independently withheld policy-level evaluation.” Policies are redeployed for new stochastic online rounds, compared with a fixed-policy baseline; the paper reports their live task outcomes (P:317–318, :327–344, :423–429, :551–610). That is empirical transfer evidence, not a universal guarantee or an independent holdout. Section 5.1 compares guided and unguided discovery; it does not establish that the guidance had won an offline replay evaluation before losing live (P:554–570). N:91–93 adds that unsupported premise, and N:296–298 turns an interpretation about compression into an experimentally established cause.

**No online reproduction contract:** accepted in the narrow deterministic sense. The online transition is expressly stochastic, whereas offline replay is deterministic (P:217–263). Appendix B publishes prompts “for reproducibility” (P:890–893), so “no reproduction material” would be wrong. “No stated seed/program/manifest contract that exactly regenerates the online tree” is supported.

**Candidate §4.4:** keep a computed disclosure, but define the denominators. Count unsupported **plans** separately from illegal **probes**, record voluntary stop versus support exhaustion versus budget termination, and report revealed-node coverage with identities and score components. A batch-decision count and a requested-probe count are not interchangeable (P:209–216, :983–989, :1163–1169). A valid early stop can reveal a small fraction and win legitimately; a small fraction is a scope fact, not automatic rejection. For ADR-004, a partial run remains unscored unless a shorter entry is separately admitted (A:1095–1099).

**Candidate §4.5:** accepted. R:78 asks for withheld families/implementations as well as fresh seeds and live transfer. A:1350–1355 already defines exposure in terms of both entries and results; A:1416 schedules new held-out sessions. Keep the training-history incumbent floor and the independent holdout as two distinct evaluations. Merely assigning another date window does not meet R's unfamiliar-family requirement. Also replace N:459's “opposite”: solver-level holdouts and policy-level holdouts are complementary.

## 7. TL;DR 6 — §4.2 is already bounded by D3, and its examples break the bound

**D3 already names the proposed property.** It admits internal computation changes expected to leave every provider call's **raw message list** and tool invocation unchanged, not merely the canonical projection. Intent is declared and reviewed; the envelope says what comparisons can and cannot establish (A:921–933, :1073–1103). This is the proposed “inside” criterion, in a stronger, existing form.

**The examples do not imply the property.** Eliding a message changes `msg_count`; changing compaction or truncation can change the sent payload while leaving the interaction's causal identity unchanged. The evaluator's actual `provider_projection` includes model, message count, canonical payload, system prefix and raw frame (`src/eval/journal/seams.ail:152–153`; `src/eval/journal/digests.ail:28–36`). Strict comparison rejects differing projection strings (`src/core/dst_replay.ail:334–365`). T0 excludes compaction and checkpoints; D7 says message-changing compaction normally fails the source request check (A:623, :1025, :1361, :1367–1370). A behavior-preserving implementation improvement to a validator might qualify; the phrase “checkpoint-chain validator” cannot establish qualification or meaningful T0 exercise by itself.

**Identity equality is necessary, not sufficient.** N:408–410 makes ordered consumption imply exact scoring and no divergence. K2 checks identity, projections and outcomes; K3–K7 independently check witnesses, invariants/census, frames, end and transcript (A:1036–1043). A bug in a candidate admitted by intent can still diverge. An admission classifier is not a semantic proof.

**The proposed outside mode is unauthorized.** D3 refuses prompt, tool-catalogue and extension changes before execution. A new journal entry does not waive that class restriction; expanding it needs a reviewed basis/tier change (A:927–933, :1271–1276, :1345–1348, :1359–1365). Regression replay is available for diagnosis and **never scores** (A:1070–1071). New seeded discovery can supply different scoped evidence but does not validate old model responses for new requests (R:53).

**The quality upgrade is expressly excluded.** D4 says no task-quality or tool-choice-quality claims because responses are recorded (A:1114). Preserving a request can preserve an observed workload; it does not show a new policy chooses better actions on novel tasks. Delete the promised bounded quality term unless a separate objective and independent evaluation are designed.

The surviving candidate is small: report the existing D3 classification, its reviewed assumptions, refusal reasons, and observed K0–K7/envelope results. It supplies neither a new replay mode nor a quality guarantee. Counting `Reproduced` versus `Diverged` as suggested at N:528–530 also cannot recover “what fraction of all candidates are inside”: inadmissible candidates are refused before execution, and admissible buggy candidates can diverge (A:927–933, :1059–1071).

### 8.3. Refusal versus silence

**“Silent zero” is an overreach.** The note combines three different cases: a legal node with no recorded child, a grid plan beyond recorded structural support, and a Motoko candidate violating replay/admission requirements. P:253–257 permits an empty child and explicitly terminates when no recorded continuation remains. P:1167–1169 says an unsupported plan cannot earn replay reward, without specifying a numeric sentinel, diagnostic format, or silent continuation. A run need not continue after exhaustion, and P:1212–1218 describes decision traces that can expose premature stops and wasted probes.

Equation (1) does not imply either asserted numerical consequence. The saved calculation (`SCRATCH/audit_evidence.py`, output `SCRATCH/audit-evidence.log`) gives:

| Calculation | Output and implication |
|---|---|
| Best score 10, beta1=1, beta2=0; 2 versus 3 revealed attempts | `8.0` versus `7.0`: a shorter record can score **higher**, not necessarily lower. This is arithmetic, not a simulated missing-worker incident. |
| Best score 10, N=2, beta1=1, beta2=0; k=2 versus k=3 | `8.0` versus `8.0`: if an empty continuation spends a round, it need not change the score, much less zero it. |

| Same best and N, beta1=1, beta2=1; k=1 versus k=2 | `10.0` versus `9.0`: N:320–323 also misses the round-dependent third term. Equal count and maximum do not force equal reward. |

The implementation of unsupported-plan handling is unknown, so these examples refute derivation from equation (1); they do not purport to execute that unpublished implementation.

**Motoko's side is accurate at the contract level, but combines distinct result types.** D:769–772 lists wrong kind/origin, unsafe identity, exhaustion and unused interactions as fatal. The replay module represents these as `WrongKind`, `WrongOrigin`, `UnsafeIdentity`, `ProgramExhausted`, `UnusedInteraction`; exhaustion maps to `InteractionsExhausted`, the other comparison failures to `ProgramMismatch` (`src/core/dst_replay.ail:137–144,209–241`). `HarnessFailure` additionally includes `InvalidProgram`, `RoutingViolation`, and `SetupFailure`; raw capability bypass can be a nonzero process exit rather than a fabricated typed result (`src/core/dst_result.ail:11–23,74–79`; D:781–795).

Thus the note is right about an explicit refusal/divergence vocabulary and no credited partial resource replay. It is wrong to present these as interchangeable constructors of one generic out-of-support result or to assume every projection change is fatal under D2 regression.

1. A durable attempt ledger, including failed/incomplete attempts, with run/window/shard and complete seed/configuration identity, program keys when available, and a reconciliation against independent attempted/completed counts (D:2355–2362; existing aggregate fields above).

Branch-union gain per seed is a coverage proxy; the direction doc's stronger target is actual defect detection per compute, with independently faulty and known-correct cases (R:59–65). An ADR-004 real-journal gate is not a prerequisite for this separate generated-axis experiment (D:2348–2374; R:59).

### 8.7. What remains of the inside/outside candidate?

The request-preservation distinction is already present in D3 **in substance and more precisely**: only internal changes expected to preserve raw provider messages and tool invocations are admissible; prompt, catalogue, model, parameter and extension changes are refused. Candidate §4.2 therefore collapses to an explicit report of **existing admissibility, refusal reasons, declared assumptions and observed envelope/check results**. ### 8.8. A cheaper, stronger candidate not proposed in the note

Before building a durable replay-training history, compare the incumbent window with one hand-written prefix scheduler on a small, independently withheld bank of faulty **and known-correct** implementations.
Keep oracle, generator configuration and compute budget fixed; run both schedulers with paired seeds, and report detected defects, missed defects, false rejections and cost.
