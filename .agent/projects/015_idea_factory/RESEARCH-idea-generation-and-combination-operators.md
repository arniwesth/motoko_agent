## 0. The question

Most of Motoko's research corpus was generated by one informal move: *"can X be combined with,
or play a role in, the Motoko architecture?"* — Tsetlin Machines × AILANG, TRM × context
compression, GRPO × Gemma4, probabilistic symbolic execution, F# transpiler feasibility
(`ls .agent/research`, ~26 entries). The factory note formalizes how ideas are *evaluated*
once they exist; it treats generation only in passing (§3.1's join rule). This note asks:

1. **What generation moves actually produced the existing corpus?** (§1)
2. **What is the right formalization target when an LLM is the generator?** (§2)
3. **What representation makes idea *combination* a computable operation rather than a
   narrative?** (§3)

The claim, in one sentence: the corpus was produced by roughly five distinct generators;
formalization should mean **naming them, forcing each to emit its characteristic artifact,
and routing everything through the factory note's §5 ledger** — so combination becomes a
query and the factory's §3.5 calibration record accrues *per operator*. The creative act
stays with the LLM/human; the formal system makes the search space enumerable and the
outcomes attributable.

---

## 1. Five generators, reverse-engineered from the corpus

Each is a distinct move with distinct preconditions, failure modes, and — most importantly —
a distinct characteristic artifact. "Idea-source" in the factory note's §3.5 calibration
becomes much sharper as this closed vocabulary than as free-text provenance.

### 1.1 JOIN — recorded weakness × newly available capability

The one generator the factory note already formalizes (§3.1). Archetype: 012's pull-driven
adoption, "registered debt × changelog delta." Arrives with a testable claim attached; dies
cheaply when the probe fails. Characteristic artifact: **a join record** naming the evidence
row and the capability delta.

### 1.2 MAP — external concept → architecture, via role decomposition

The dominant generator in the corpus, and the one behind "can X play a role in Motoko."
Crucially it is *not* a join: there is often no recorded weakness on the Motoko side when it
starts. What 014 actually did with the Gödel-machine papers was decompose X into its
mechanism roles (archive, selection, mutation operator, admission gate) and ask, per role,
"what fills this in Motoko?" This is structure-mapping (Gentner) done informally: map
*relational structure*, not surface features; the mapping's residue — roles with no filler —
is the idea output. Characteristic artifact: **a role-by-role gap table** (014's was the
load-bearing part of that note).

### 1.3 LIFT — apply an existing idea one level up or down

The factory note itself is 014 lifted one level ("ask the same question where candidates are
research directions rather than diffs"). Its §2 observation that "the factory is the general
case of the mutation operator" is a lift discovered after the fact. Characteristic artifact:
**a level-correspondence argument** (factory note §2's degree table is one).

### 1.4 MERGE — detect that N existing proposals are views of one object

Factory note §5 is a merge in progress: debt registry ≡ outcome matrix ≡ idea queue. The
purest form of "combining ideas," driven by isomorphism detection rather than need.
Characteristic artifact: **an isomorphism argument plus a unification proposal**.

### 1.5 OBSERVE — an idea whose payoff is a new measurement axis

Project 011 (mutation kill-rates per invariant family). Not a combination at all; this is the
factory note's §3.2 exploration-budget case, listed here so the operator vocabulary is
closed over the corpus. Characteristic artifact: **a claimed new oracle plus what it would
have caught**.

## 2. The reframe: with an LLM in the loop, generation is not the scarce resource

Classical creativity formalization (TRIZ, Swanson, computational creativity) assumed
generating candidate combinations was hard. That is inverted now: a Claude session can
produce fifty plausible "X × Motoko" candidates with fluent narratives in one sitting. The
scarce resources are:

1. **An enumerable, pruned search space** — so generation is systematic rather than driven by
   whichever paper was read this week;
2. **A cheap admissibility test** — so most candidates die before a research note is written
   (factory note §3.3);
3. **Provenance** — so the factory learns which regions of the space, and which operators,
   pay off (factory note §3.5).

So the formalization target is **not a machine that creates ideas** — the LLM is that
machine, and it is fine. The target is the representation that makes the combination space
enumerable and the operators auditable. This is a different — and more tractable — design
problem than the one the classical literature was solving.

## 3. Representation: ideas with typed interfaces

For combination to be more than vibes, each idea/concept needs a small interface record —
not an ontology, just enough structure to compute joins over:

- **mechanism**: the roles and relations that make it work (what MAP maps against)
- **requires**: capabilities/preconditions it needs from the host
- **provides**: capabilities or *measurement axes* it adds
- **evidence**: ledger rows it joins against (may be empty for MAP-generated ideas)
- **open questions**: its unresolved sockets

Two ideas are combinable when their interfaces compose, and there are only a few
composition patterns:

1. **A provides what B requires** — pipeline composition. E.g. 014's G1 keyed matrix
   *provides* the lineage data that HGM-style clade selection *requires*; the factory note's
   §6 Q2 already notices this dependency without naming the pattern.
2. **A and B share a mechanism core** — merge candidate (the §5-ledger case).
3. **A answers an open question of B** — socket-fill.

**Socket-fill is the highest-leverage cheap move available right now.** Every research note
in the tree already ends with an open-questions section; those are typed sockets, and nothing
indexes them. When a new paper or capability X arrives, the highest-precision generation
query is not "can X be combined with Motoko?" (too broad — always answerable with a
narrative) but "does X answer any standing open question in the corpus?" That is a join
against an index that almost exists, it inherits the low-false-positive economics the factory
note attributes to joins, and the resulting idea arrives pre-attached to the context that
will evaluate it. An open-questions index extracted from existing notes is a
"schema field + derived index" move, exactly the shape factory note §4 prefers.

## 4. Precedents — steal selectively

- **TRIZ** is the closest structural precedent to the JOIN rule, and encouraging: Altshuller
  derived, from a patent corpus, a matrix of (contradiction type) × (inventive principle) —
  a *weakness taxonomy* crossed with a *mechanism library*, populated empirically from
  settled outcomes. The Motoko analogue writes itself: weakness classes (oracle blindness,
  staleness, unmeasured gate, missing lineage…) × mechanism families (keying discipline,
  report-only advisor, gate-then-score, staged commitment…), cells populated by which
  pairings landed. The catch: TRIZ took thousands of patents to populate; Motoko has ~16
  projects. So: design the record-keeping now, expect the matrix to be useful in years.
- **Swanson ABC linking** (literature-based discovery) is the formal ancestor of JOIN — A
  relates to B, B to C, nobody has stated A–C. Its 40-year record teaches one hard lesson:
  the linking step is trivial to automate and drowns in false positives; everything is in
  pruning and downstream validation. Which is the factory note's gate-then-score criterion —
  the generator can be dumb if the admissions gate is measured.
- **Boden's combinational vs transformational distinction** marks the ceiling: combination
  operators over a fixed representation cannot produce ideas that change the representation
  itself. The move from "Motoko as a program" to "Motoko as a population with lineage" (014)
  was not a combination in the old vocabulary — it changed what the vocabulary quantifies
  over. This is factory note §6 Q4 in different clothes: the operator set is itself an
  oracle-like object under the same epoch rule, and expecting the representation to be
  periodically broken *from outside* is permanently degree-0 human work.

## 5. Proposed moves, ranked by the factory note's own §3.3 criterion

Cheap experiment first, residual value even if nothing downstream happens:

1. **Make the operator vocabulary the provenance field.** Every new research note states
   which generator produced it (JOIN / MAP / LIFT / MERGE / OBSERVE) plus its §3.5
   prediction. Zero new tooling; one line of frontmatter; enables per-operator calibration
   later.
2. **Standardize the gap table as MAP's mandatory artifact.** A MAP-generated idea without a
   role-by-role gap table is "a sentiment, not a proposal" — the same rule §3.4 applies to
   acceptance criteria, applied to the mapping itself. Bonus: unfilled roles in a gap table
   are *new ledger rows*, which means **MAP feeds JOIN** — the two generators compose, and
   the §5 ledger is the composition point.
3. **Index the open questions** (§3's socket-fill). Cheap, and pure-documentation residual
   value even if never used generatively.

## 6. Tensions to keep visible

1. **Goodhart on the generator.** Factory note §6 Q4's worry ("manufacturing recorded
   evidence to join against") gets *worse* once operators are named, because "dress an
   intuition up as a JOIN" becomes the obvious move for any generator — human or LLM — that
   knows JOINs are queued first. §3.1's pressure valve (evidence-less ideas are queued
   behind, not rejected) must stay.
2. **MAP has no natural kill signal.** A JOIN dies when its probe fails. A MAP can always
   produce a gap table that reads as interesting — and the corpus shows it: many research
   entries are MAPs that went nowhere visible (Tsetlin machines, omnigraph, spatiotemporal
   composability), and per factory note §1 obs. 3 nothing records whether they were killed
   or are merely dormant. Formalizing MAP without a kill criterion industrializes the
   production of intriguing shelf-ware. Candidate rule, in the house style: *a MAP whose gap
   table produces no row that converts to a ledger entry or an open-question fill within one
   epoch is auto-killed, with a trace.*
3. **Don't formalize past the corpus size.** With ~16 projects and ~26 research entries,
   anything heavier than frontmatter conventions and one index is a dashboard before the
   conventions exist — factory note §7's own warning.

---

## 7. Open questions

1. **Is MERGE automatable at all?** It found the §5 unification — arguably the
   highest-value single insight in the recent notes — but it required holding three
   proposals in one head simultaneously, which is exactly what a per-session LLM factory is
   structurally worst at. This may be the strongest argument for the ledger being one
   physical object rather than three views.
2. **Is the operator vocabulary closed?** Five operators cover the current corpus, but the
   corpus is small. The test: when a new research note doesn't fit any operator, is that a
   sixth operator or a transformational event (§4, Boden) outside the vocabulary by
   construction?
3. **What does the interface record (§3) cost in practice?** If filling in
   mechanism/requires/provides per idea takes longer than writing the idea, the discipline
   dies. The minimal viable version may be just `evidence:` + `open-questions:` — the two
   fields the composition patterns actually query.
4. **Does per-operator calibration need a minimum n before it's signal?** With ~2–5
   settled outcomes per operator for years, the calibration record may be noise dressed as
   learning — the same staleness risk `measure-movement-not-comfort` warns about.

---

## 8. Explicitly out of scope for now

- **Automating any generator.** Same posture as the factory note §7: this note makes the
  manual moves nameable and auditable; automation decisions come after 014's WI-1/WI-2
  produce data.
- **Building the interface-record schema as tooling.** §5's moves are frontmatter
  conventions plus one derived index; nothing more until the conventions have been exercised.
- **Retro-tagging the existing corpus with operators.** Useful as a calibration bootstrap
  (factory note §6 Q5) but a separate decision, not implied by adopting the vocabulary for
  new notes.
